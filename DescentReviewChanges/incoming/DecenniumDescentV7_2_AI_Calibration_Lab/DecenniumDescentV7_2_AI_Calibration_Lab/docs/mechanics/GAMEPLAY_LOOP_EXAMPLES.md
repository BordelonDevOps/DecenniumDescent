# Gameplay Loop Worked Examples

## Example Expedition: The Brass Lantern Company

The Brass Lantern Company is a twelve-person expedition attempting to reach the Haven with a profitable cargo and verify a damaged route beyond it.

### Roster and offices

| Character | Class or specialty | Office | Initial team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mara Venn | Soldier | Expedition Leader | Combat |
| Orrik Stonewake | Warrior | — | Combat |
| Salira Dusk | Thief | Routekeeper | Reconnaissance |
| Pell Quickstep | Ranger | — | Reconnaissance |
| Edrin Vale | Wizard | Claimant of Record | Research |
| Sister Maelin | Cleric | Surgeon | Combat |
| Zeetes | Tinker | Quartermaster | Support |
| Harka Reed | Druid | — | Resource |
| Brannic Forgehand | Dwarf smith | — | Resource |
| Ilse Harrow | Porter captain | — | Support |
| Toma Glass | Alchemist | — | Research |
| Taran Ashwall | Paladin | — | Combat |

The company records a primary objective: deliver licensed medicines to the Haven and return with at least 8,000 gp in portable value. Secondary objectives are to verify the Copper Stair route and recover Bloomdeep reagents.

Abort conditions are two Critical members, fewer than twenty-four Supply, broken primary transport, or hour 1,080 without a secured return route.

### Preparation

The company begins with:

- 144 Supply, enough for twelve Medium people for twelve days.
- A convoy capacity of 100 Load.
- 42 Load already occupied by armor, tools, medicines, and camp equipment.
- A local guild sponsor taking 20% of net proceeds.
- One temporary-camp kit.
- 600 gp reserve treasury.

The Quartermaster deliberately leaves 58 Load free. This is the first meaningful extraction decision: more tools could improve checks, but every added tool reduces profitable cargo capacity.

## Watch One: Entering Tier I

### Assignments

- Combat: Advance.
- Reconnaissance: Scout Ahead.
- Research: Identify old route marks.
- Resource: Forage.
- Support: Transport.

The route is Mapped, Length 6, Risk 1, Capacity 80, and Sustenance 2. The convoy is under Capacity.

The Reconnaissance team succeeds, learning that a scavenger pack is feeding near the eastern passage. The players choose the western passage, reducing Pressure by 1 to 0. The route gains 2 Progress.

No Pressure roll is made at Pressure 0. The Watch consumes four Supply, representing one-third of the daily requirement.

## Watch Two: A profitable detour

The Resource team asks to harvest luminous cavern moss. This changes its assignment from Forage to Harvest. Reconnaissance Maps the detour rather than scouting forward.

The Resource lead rolls 19 against DC 12, a Success. The team gains four Load of public Tier I reagents worth 160 gp. Because the cargo is Perishable, Support must Preserve it during a future Watch or it loses half value after twenty-four hours.

Reconnaissance fails to Map the detour. No Progress is gained from that activity. The route's normal travel still adds 2 Progress. Pressure remains 1 and the d6 result is 5, so no complication occurs.

## Encounter example: protecting cargo

During Watch Three, the Pressure roll triggers a Standard complication: burrowing scavengers attack the rear of the convoy.

The Director states the objective before initiative:

- The scavengers are trying to drag away the medicine crate.
- The players win if they drive off the scavengers or keep the crate secured for four rounds.
- The scavengers win if they move the crate thirty feet into a side tunnel.

This keeps the fight tied to expedition stakes. A character can spend an Action to Secure Cargo, move the crate, cut a harness, Guard a porter, or attack.

The party wins after three rounds but spends healing and gains Pressure because of the noise. They do not receive extra XP for killing the final fleeing scavenger; the award is based on the Standard encounter and the secured objective.

## Camp example

At the end of the first day, nine characters take a Rest Watch while three Support and Reconnaissance members establish a Temporary Camp and conceal its approaches.

The Support check succeeds. The camp removes the exposure-based Pressure increase and safely stores twenty Load. The Reconnaissance check Critically Succeeds, reducing Pressure and creating a concealed escape route.

The three working characters each record Rest Debt with a two-Watch countdown. If one works through both following Watches without completing a full Rest Watch, that character gains 1 Strain at the end of the second Watch and still owes the Rest. The active roster therefore contains four Combat members, two Reconnaissance members, two Research members, two Resource members, and two Support members. The system makes that scheduling choice visible without requiring twelve simultaneous combatants.

## Unknown-route forced march example

A Reconnaissance team fails Map Route on an Unknown spur while the Expedition has only one Watch before a contract deadline. The leader may wait and try Reconnaissance again, or declare a Forced March.

The Routekeeper attempts a Severe route check. On Success, the Expedition gains 1 Progress, Pressure increases by 2, and every active character consumes one additional Supply. On Failure, it gains no Progress and faces a Standard complication. This option prevents the route from becoming mechanically impossible while making the cost of moving without reliable reconnaissance explicit.

## Extraction example

The company reaches the Haven, fulfills the medicine contract, and later returns toward the surface with:

| Cargo | Load | Extraction Class | Priority | Base value |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Documented Bloomdeep catalyst | 8 | 2 | 5 | 3,000 gp |
| Preserved luminous moss | 4 | 1 | 2 | 160 gp |
| Damaged pre-Cycle lens | 6 | 3 | 4 | 4,500 gp |
| Common metal salvage | 20 | 1 | 1 | 800 gp |
| Rescued surveyor | 6 | 3 | 5 | No sale value |

Highest Extraction Class is 3, so declaring extraction raises Pressure by 1. The Extraction Test begins at the tier DC +3 for Extraction Class. The convoy is under Capacity, but the Fragile lens and Living surveyor each add +1 if not properly secured; cargo-and-handling modifiers cannot exceed +7 before pursuit and route modifiers.

At the first route transition, the Quartermaster's test fails. The Director states the stakes: lose eight hours and face a Standard complication, or risk one priority-1 cargo lot. The players abandon half the common metal salvage rather than risk the Horn clock. Their choice is informed and visible; the Director does not secretly erase random treasure.

## Tier I Haven fast-settlement example

The Expedition returns with ordinary documented reagents, no quarantine concern, no disputed claim, and a local-guild sponsor. It uses the fast path: Inspection, Appraisal, Sale, Sponsor Settlement, and Recovery. The full ten-step procedure is not performed merely for ceremony. If appraisal reveals contraband or a competing lien, the return escalates to the relevant full Haven steps.

## Haven appraisal and sale example

The lens has a Base Value of 4,500 gp.

- Provenance is documented: ×1.0.
- Demand is sought: ×1.25.
- Condition is damaged: ×0.5.
- The company uses a patient auction: ×1.1.

Final Value is 4,500 × 1.0 × 1.25 × 0.5 × 1.1 = 3,094 gp after rounding.

The lens is an artifact, so the Haven applies the 6% artifact tax: 3,094 × 0.94 = 2,908 gp after rounding. The guild sponsor receives 20%, or 582 gp. The Expedition retains 2,326 gp before wages, repairs, replacement equipment, debt service, and personal shares. This is net expedition proceeds, not yet true profit.

## Retreat example

A Tier III expedition encounters a creature it cannot reliably damage. The Expedition declares Retreat.

Round one: the Ranger leads with Survival and succeeds, earning one Escape success. The Soldier Guards the rear, and the Tinker deploys an obstacle.

Round two: the creature wins the exchange and destroys the obstacle, earning one Pursuit success. The party abandons a low-priority salvage sled, gaining advantage on the next Escape check.

Round three: the Thief uses a mapped side passage and Critically Succeeds. A Critical Success grants two Escape successes, completing the retreat three to one. The party does not also add another success for winning the round because the normal cap is one Escape source per round; the Critical Success itself is the explicit exception.

They return to Expedition Mode with Pressure +1, a lost sled, and a reason to research a counter. The retreat is a playable outcome rather than an improvised total-party kill.

## Optional detailed XP example

This table has chosen optional detailed XP instead of milestone advancement. A level 5 character has a 9,000 XP gap to level 6 under the existing advancement table.

During the expedition, that character materially contributes to:

- One Standard encounter: 10%, or 900 XP.
- One major route discovery: 10%, or 900 XP.
- The primary expedition objective: 25%, or 2,250 XP.
- Profitable extraction: 10%, or 900 XP.

Total award is 4,950 XP. The Research, Resource, Reconnaissance, and Support members receive the same objective awards when they materially contributed. The combatants do not monopolize advancement.

# Director Quick Procedures

## Creating a route

1. Choose Length from 3–12 Progress.
2. Set Risk from 1–5.
3. Choose Mapping state.
4. Set Capacity according to the narrowest passage or transport limitation.
5. Set Sustenance from 0–4.
6. Write one expected complication, one hidden opportunity, and one retreat path.

## Creating a Watch complication

A complication should threaten one of five things:

- Time.
- Supply.
- Position.
- Cargo or transport.
- Information or secrecy.

It does not always need to become combat. Use negotiation, environmental hazards, lost routes, faction demands, disease, theft, equipment failure, or contradictory evidence.

## Building an encounter

1. Choose a budget.
2. Add enemy Threat Points.
3. Add active hazard, hostile terrain, surprise, or protected-cargo adjustments only if they were not already used to choose the final label. Never double-step.
4. State the objective, clock, and failure result. Escort and Retreat difficulty are measured by their clocks, not kill win rate alone.
5. Add at least one exploitable terrain element.
6. Decide whether the enemy retreats, bargains, hunts, feeds, captures, or fights to death.

## Resolving a cargo loss

1. State which cargo is at risk.
2. Respect the recorded priority order.
3. Offer a costed response: time, Supply, combat position, injury, alternate cargo, or debt.
4. Record the provenance and financial consequence.
5. Never remove a major item without a visible cause and player decision unless the table accepted a random-loss procedure in advance.

# Quick Reference Tables

## One Watch

1. Confirm assignments.
2. Consume one-third of daily Supply.
3. Resolve team activities.
4. Add route Progress.
5. Apply Pressure changes.
6. Roll the Pressure d6.
7. Resolve complications.
8. Update cargo, Strain, Wounds, and Integrity.
9. Advance eight hours.

## One Haven Turn

1. Choose one downtime activity per character.
2. Resolve required checks and costs.
3. Advance one day.
4. Update recovery, crafting, research, business, and faction clocks.
5. Resolve one Haven event when appropriate.

## End of session

- Opening hour and Watch.
- Location and route state.
- Pressure and camp.
- Supply and treasury.
- Member assignments, Strain, and Wounds.
- Cargo manifest and priority.
- Active objective and abort conditions.
- Faction changes and unresolved threats.
