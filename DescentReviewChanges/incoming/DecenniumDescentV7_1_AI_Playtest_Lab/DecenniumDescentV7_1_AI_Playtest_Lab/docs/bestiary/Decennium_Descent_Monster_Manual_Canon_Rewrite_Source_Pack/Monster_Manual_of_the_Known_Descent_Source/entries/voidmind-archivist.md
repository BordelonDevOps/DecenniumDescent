## What It Is

Voidmind Archivist treats distance and possession as negotiable states. It can make separation feel like movement and can turn an apparently empty container, corridor, or memory into an attack vector.

## What Experienced Expeditions Notice

Shadows pointing toward different lights; containers heavier when empty; spoken names missing from echoes; objects arriving before the hand releases them. Different witnesses remember the same sound differently.

## How It Fights

It opens with **Void Rend** against the target most capable of controlling its movement. It isolates one target and makes the rest of the party spend actions proving where that target is.

## How to Defeat It

**Prepare Before Contact**

- Prepare resistance or mitigation for psychic damage.
- Tether the team to stable anchor points.

**Combat Priority**

Protect memory and positioning; force the creature to act inside anchored space.

**Exploit**

The defining weakness is **stable anchors and force wards**. Treat this as an encounter solution rather than an automatic damage vulnerability. Successful Research, Recon, or Resource actions should make the exploit easier or safer.

**Withdraw When**

Withdraw or change the objective when the creature reaches favorable terrain or isolates a target. A failed retreat against this creature usually costs more than abandoning the immediate treasure.

## Habitat, Goals, and Lair

Void sites contain anchor stones, folded corridors, suspended debris, and pockets where sound or light fails. Destroying an anchor can open a worse route.

Void entities manipulate distance, ownership, memory, and absence. Some are predators; others are collectors or refugees whose presence makes ordinary navigation unreliable.

Brokers value unique experiences and impossible cargo. Archivists respect accurate records. Drifters may respond to restored light paths.
