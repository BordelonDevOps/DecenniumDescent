## What It Is

Crystal Shardling is a dungeon-adapted predator or hazard. Its behavior remains rooted in food, territory, nesting, and escape, which gives a prepared Expedition more options than a direct fight.

## What Experienced Expeditions Notice

Scratches at the creature’s true height; prey dragged away rather than consumed in place; mineral dust in droppings; repeated avoidance of a seemingly safe route.

### Actions

**Multiattack.** The creature makes 2 attacks using the actions below.

**Crystal Spike.** *Attack:* +5 to hit, one target. *Hit:* 1d8+3 piercing damage (average 8).

**Resonant Shard.** *Attack:* +5 to hit, one target. *Hit:* 1d6+3 force damage (average 6).

**Shatterburst (Recharge 5-6).** Creatures in the affected area make a DC 13 Dexterity save, taking 3d6 force damage (average 10) on a failure and half on a success. The Director should describe the charge-up before resolving it.

## How It Fights

It opens with **Crystal Spike** against the target most capable of controlling its movement. It uses **Resonant Shard** to punish anyone who tries to stabilize that target. When **Shatterburst** recharges, it delays only if it can catch a more important cluster or interrupt a decisive ritual. It attacks from concealment, protects its escape path, and stops pursuing once territory or food is secured.

## How to Defeat It

**Prepare Before Contact**

- Prepare resistance or mitigation for piercing, force damage.
- Spread before **Shatterburst** becomes available.
- Carry bait, scent control, and a noncombat retreat route.

**Combat Priority**

Control its escape and feeding choices rather than cornering it.

**Exploit**

The defining weakness is **None identified**. Treat this as an encounter solution rather than an automatic damage vulnerability. Successful Research, Recon, or Resource actions should make the exploit easier or safer.

**Withdraw When**

Withdraw or change the objective when Shatterburst catches three or more Expedition members. A failed retreat against this creature usually costs more than abandoning the immediate treasure.

## Habitat, Goals, and Lair

Predators choose terrain that hides approach and protects retreat. The lair is usually more dangerous than the animal.

Not every dangerous creature serves a faction or cosmic principle. Ordinary predators become expedition-level threats when dungeon terrain, repeated Openings, or magical food chains reshape them.

Animal Handling, food, territory, and nonthreatening movement matter more than language.
