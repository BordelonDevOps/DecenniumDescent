# Newcomer Player Guide — Decennium Descent

**Audience:** First-time players at the table (or in a playtest). You do not need D&D experience to start — but if you have some, a few things here will look familiar and a few will deliberately work differently.

**Companion app:** Build your character in **CHARACTERS**, track fights in **SESSION**. The app helps with math and labels; the **Director** still decides what happens in the story.

---

## Your turn — in plain language

Combat runs in **rounds** (~6 seconds of fiction). Everyone acts once per round, in **initiative order** (roll d20 + Dexterity modifier; higher goes first).

When it's **your turn**, you get a small menu of things to spend. You don't have to use everything — but you usually want to do *something* useful.

| Thing | Plain English | Typical use |
|-------|---------------|-------------|
| **Move** | Walk, run, climb — up to your **Speed** (often 30 ft). You can split this before and after your main action. | Get in range, get behind cover, back away from danger. |
| **Action** | Your big "main thing" this turn — **one per turn**. | Attack, cast most spells, **Help** an ally, **Dodge**, **Dash**, use a class feature that says "action." |
| **Bonus action** | A quick extra — **one per turn**, only if you have something that grants one. | Some spells (e.g. *Healing Word*), class tricks (Bardic flourishes, Thief tricks), certain weapon styles. |
| **Reaction** | A snap response to someone else's action — **one per round**, refreshes at the start of your turn. | Opportunity attack when a foe runs away, some class defenses (e.g. Monk **Deflect Missile**). |
| **Free interaction** | One small object or environment thing. | Draw a weapon, open a door, flip a lever. A second object usually costs your **Action**. |

**Order is flexible:** You can move, then attack, then keep moving — or attack first and then run. You can also skip anything you don't need.

**Surprised?** If the GM says you were caught off guard, you can't move or act on your first turn and can't use a **Reaction** until that turn ends.

**Out of combat:** The same action/bonus/reaction ideas still apply when the Director calls for structured time — but most exploration is "describe what you do" and the Director sets the pace.

## Magic: Resonance + Dissonance

If you play a **Weave caster**, you track two things:

1. **Resonance Points (RP)** — your available magical power.
2. **Dissonance** — strain caused by forcing the Weave, especially through repetition and Overcast.

A spell's **Resonance Rank** sets its base cost: `Rank × 2 RP`; cantrips cost 0 RP. Rank is not a universal character-level gate. Two classes can learn the same spell at different levels because each class is taught a different magical cadence.

Repeating the same named spell immediately builds **Refrain** Dissonance: +1, then +2, then +3 maximum on later repeats. Yes, repeated cantrips build Refrain too. Casting a different spell breaks the Refrain chain.

At higher Dissonance you become **Frayed**, then **Discordant**, then **Fractured**. Costs rise; ordinary casters eventually lose Overcast and then ranked casting until they reduce Dissonance. This is why the best caster does not simply spam the mathematically strongest spell every turn.

You reduce Dissonance by changing spells, letting a turn pass without Weave magic, using **Ground the Note**, or taking a **Retune**. Your class may have its own safe way to vent or redirect a small amount of strain.

**Low Resonance** (below 25% RP) just means your power reserve is nearly empty. It is not a separate magical injury rule.

Read [`RESONANCE_CHEATSHEET.md`](./RESONANCE_CHEATSHEET.md) at the table.

**Dark-path exception:** Lich and Necromancer can force magic through Fractured Dissonance, but they take direct **Traumatic Casting** backlash for doing it.

## Oracle? Use RC, not RP

**Oracle** is a divine class, but it does **not** use Resonance Points.

| | Weave casters | Oracle |
|---|---------------|--------|
| Resource | **Resonance Points (RP)** | **Revelation Charges (RC)** |
| Starts at | Class/level table | **3 RC** at level 1, up to **7** at higher levels |
| Spent on | Prepared Weave spells | **Mystery Revelations** (your Mystery's special powers) |
| Gets them back via | Retune / full harmony (rests) | Each Mystery's **Prophecy Trigger** — often **story or omen driven**, not "I short rest" |

**Don't tell an Oracle player to "save a slot."** Ask what RC they have and what their Mystery does.

The companion **Session** tab may bump RC +1 on a short rest as a convenience — at a strict table, confirm with your GM whether rests recover RC or only Prophecy Triggers do.

## Rests — when to catch your breath

| Rest | What comes back |
|---|---|
| **Retune (short rest)** | About ¼ max RP + casting stat modifier; **Dissonance resets to 0**. Class features refresh only when their text says so. |
| **Full Harmony (long rest)** | RP returns to maximum and Dissonance resets to 0. Oracle follows its own RC/Mystery rules. |

**Dungeon clock:** During the Great Descent, the full Opening lasts **100 days / 2,400 hours**. The Horn sounds at **day 50 / hour 1,200**, the point-of-no-return warning. A long rest still consumes real expedition time.

**Between fights:** do not assume a short rest refills your spell power. Retune is partial RP recovery even though it fully clears Dissonance.

## Death saves — you're not dead at 0 HP (usually)

When damage drops you to **0 HP**:

1. You fall **unconscious** (unless you die instantly — see below).
2. At the **start of each of your turns**, roll a **death saving throw** (d20, no modifier unless a feature says otherwise):
   - **10+** = one success  
   - **9 or lower** = one failure  
   - **3 successes** = **stable** (still unconscious, but not dying)  
   - **3 failures** = **dead**  
   - Natural **1** = two failures  
   - Natural **20** = you wake with **1 HP**

**Instant death:** If the damage that hit 0 also equals or exceeds your **maximum HP**, you die outright — no saves.

**While you're down:** Any damage = one failed death save (two on a crit). **Healing** that restores any HP ends the death-save track and wakes you up.

**Helping a downed ally:**

| Method | What you do |
|--------|-------------|
| **Healing magic** | *Healing Word* (bonus action), *Cure Wounds*, potion, etc. — restores HP, ends saves. |
| **Stabilize** | **Action** + DC 10 **Medicine** check — ally stops making death saves but stays at 0 HP until healed. |
| **Help (in combat)** | **Action**: give an ally **advantage** on their **next attack** against a foe within 5 ft of you — not a death-save button, but great for setting up a kill before someone else drops. |
| **Protect** | Body-block, **Dodge**, drag them behind cover — narrative + mechanics together. |

The companion **Session** tracker shows HP at 0 but **does not roll death saves for you**. Call out successes and failures at the table (paper tally or ask the GM to track).

## Picking your first race and class

You have **14 peoples** and **17 classes** in the companion — plenty of room for a character you *want* to play. A few honest notes for your **first** hero:

### Easy to recommend for newcomers

| Pick | Why |
|------|-----|
| **Dwarf Cleric** (or **Mountain Dwarf**) | Tough, armored, heals the party. Cleric has a full-caster RP curve and a clear Conviction support loop. Classic support role — see playtest sheet `characters/P2_CASUAL_5E.md`. |
| **Human / Dwarf / Troll Warrior or Soldier** | Straightforward "stand in front, hit things." **No RP pool** to learn on day one. |
| **Troll → Warrior** | Playtest sims rank **Troll + Warrior** among the sturdiest combos (high HP, good AC, strong damage). Good if you want to stay alive while learning rules. |

### Fine, but know the tradeoffs

| Pick | Caveat |
|------|--------|
| **Umbrin** (any class) | Cool lore, strong darkvision — but **Sunlight Sensitivity** hurts on **lit surface** adventures. See below. |
| **Bard / Mage / Wizard** | Fun and flexible — but **RP budgeting** is the main rules hurdle. Read the cheat sheet. |
| **Oracle** | Powerful narrative class — learn **RC + Mystery**, not RP. More GM conversation up front. |

### "Trap" combos (not forbidden — just fragile at low level)

Playtest matrix sims (`RACE_CLASS_SYNERGY_REPORT.md`, draft/provisional) flag **Gnome + Mage / Wizard / Mirrorsage** as especially squishy: very low HP (~27 at the sim's level 5 benchmark), low AC, low damage — **100% solo death-flag** in that harness. Fine for an experienced player who wants a challenge; **rough first character** if you're still learning positioning and rests.

**Human Warrior** also underperformed in the same sim — not unplayable, just not the tankiest default.

**None of this is a ban.** Pick what excites you. Just know **Constitution**, **armor**, and **party role** matter as much as cool spell names.

### Three practical tips

1. **Match class to job:** Who heals? Who stands in front? Who talks to NPCs? Spread jobs across the party.
2. **Read your racial traits** before session zero — especially **Sunlight Sensitivity**, **size**, and **darkvision**.
3. **Ask the GM** which companion labels say **(draft)** — some subclass numbers are still being finalized.

## Umbrin and sunlight

**Umbrin** are obsidian-skinned elves of **The Murk** — exile culture, Murkish language, complicated surface reputation. They're a full player people, not a villain race — but surface folk may **fear or distrust** you until you earn trust.

**Sunlight Sensitivity** (all Umbrin):

> You have **disadvantage** on attack rolls and on **Wisdom (Perception)** checks that rely on sight when you, your target, or what you're trying to see is in **direct sunlight**.

**What that means at the table:**

- Bright **outdoor daytime** fights = harder to hit and harder to spot things.  
- **Torches, overcast skies, shade, indoors, dungeons** = usually fine — confirm with the GM for edge cases.  
- If the adventure blurb says **Blackglass Reach**, **surface march**, or **open sky**, mention your sensitivity in session zero so the GM can plan (night raids, canopy cover, magical shade, etc.).

**Social note:** Umbrin aren't "evil by stat block." Expect roleplay friction with temple guards and mainstream Choir folk — and interesting alliances with underworld or scholar factions. See [`UMBRIN_LORE.md`](../mechanics/UMBRIN_LORE.md) if you want deeper lore (optional).

## Table etiquette — and what to ask the GM

Decennium Descent is a **collaborative story** with structured combat. These habits keep sessions fun:

### Do

- **Say what you're trying to do in fiction**, then ask how to roll it if you're unsure. ("I want to leap onto the wagon — is that Athletics?")  
- **Track your own HP, RP/RC, and conditions** — the companion helps; the GM owns the world.  
- **Share the spotlight** — pass plans to quieter players, celebrate others' crits.  
- **Flag confusion immediately** — "Is that my action or bonus action?" is always a fair question.  
- **Note (draft)** tags in the app and ask whether a feature is fully wired for your level.

### Don't stress about

- Perfect optimization on character #1.  
- Knowing every spell in the library.  
- MMO jargon — this is a tabletop RPG first.

### Great questions for session zero

1. "Are we using **Resonance (RP)** or should I think of spell **slots**?" → RP. Cheat sheet linked above.  
2. "Does a **short rest** give me all my magic back?" → No — **Retune** is partial.  
3. "We're playing **outside** — does my Umbrin **Sunlight Sensitivity** matter?" → Often yes in direct sun.  
4. "Our Oracle — do they use **RC** instead of RP?" → Yes.  
5. "What happens at **0 HP**?" → Unconscious + death saves unless instant death.  
6. "Is this adventure **tier X** — are we the right level?" → GM confirms.  
7. "Which companion features are **draft** for my class?" → GM or sheet badges.  
8. "If this is a playtest, can my character die?" → Director sets stakes.

### Great questions during play

- "Can I **Help** [ally] so they have advantage on that goblin?"  
- "Do I still have a **reaction** if I used one last round?" → Refreshes start of your turn.  
- "How much **RP** does that spell cost after my alignment discount?" → Cheat sheet formula; app may show preview.  
- "Can I **stabilize** them instead of attacking?" → Yes — Action + Medicine DC 10.

**When in doubt, ask the GM.** No one expects you to have Chapter 8 memorized.
