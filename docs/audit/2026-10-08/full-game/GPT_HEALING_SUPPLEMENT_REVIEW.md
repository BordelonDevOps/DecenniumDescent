A. Correct the damage vocabulary first

The first proposal was too narrow in using normal-HP loss as generic “actual damage dealt.” Use these separate quantities:

Quantity	Definition	Used for
resolvedDamage	Damage remaining after the attack/save and ordinary defenses, before temp HP	“Takes/damages” event existence, attached riders
tempAbsorbed	min(currentTempHP, resolvedDamage)	Damage actually absorbed by temp HP
postTempDamage	resolvedDamage - tempAbsorbed, uncapped by current HP	Core Instant Death and Wound threshold
preFloorHpLoss	min(preEventHP, postTempDamage)	Provisional normal-HP loss before a floor
actualHpDamage	Normal HP actually lost after a source-specific HP floor/prevention	Sources expressly saying actual HP damage/lost
actualDamageDealt	NEW anti-overkill bridge: tempAbsorbed + actualHpDamage	Numeric drain/reflection text saying actual damage dealt
positiveDamageEvent	resolvedDamage > 0	Non-numeric wording such as “damages,” “takes damage,” “damaging attack” unless the source specifies actual HP damage

This avoids two opposite errors: temporary HP still counts as actual damage dealt, while 50 points of overkill on a creature with 2 HP does not generate 50 points of life-drain healing.

Examples:

State before packet	Incoming resolved damage	Result
HP 10, temp HP 10	5	tempAbsorbed=5, postTempDamage=0, actualHpDamage=0, actualDamageDealt=5, positive-damage hooks qualify
HP 1, protected by a 1-HP floor	5	postTempDamage=5, HP remains 1, actualHpDamage=0, actualDamageDealt=0; ordinary “was damaged” hooks still see a positive packet
HP 3, no floor	12	postTempDamage=12, actualHpDamage=3, actualDamageDealt=3; Core thresholds still use 12
HP 0	1	postTempDamage=1, actualHpDamage=0, actualDamageDealt=0; zero-HP damage consequences still occur

Therefore Burning Blood and Rewind Wound, which expressly reference actual HP damage, use actualHpDamage. Sanguine Rite, Echo Leech, Merciful Smite, Wound Eater drains and Gravebrand wording based on actual damage dealt use actualDamageDealt. Ordinary predicates such as “damages the ally” should not silently become HP-loss predicates. The supplement itself reinforces that distinction in several cards.

B. Correct damage → death ordering

Preserve the closed Core stop on Instant Death.

For a damage event:

Step	Procedure
1	Snapshot HP/temp HP/Wounds/death state and resolve defenses.
2	Compute resolvedDamage, temp absorption and uncapped postTempDamage.
3	Test Core Instant Death. If true, the packet does not generate a Wound save or ordinary zero-HP death failure. Open a would-die replacement window.
4	If no Instant Death, calculate provisional HP and resolve legal damage floors such as Miracle Veil, Redundant Organs, Song of Mending, etc. Floors do not change postTempDamage.
5	Resolve the one Core Wound save if triggered. The positive→0 trigger uses the floor-adjusted result; the half-max/Maiming triggers still use the raw Core quantities.
6	If that Wound save is fatal, open would-die replacement. Do not proceed to emergency healing unless the creature survives.
7	Resolve already-zero damage failures/death and any would-die replacement.
8	Finalize a newly surviving 0-HP creature's dying/Stable/knockout state. At this narrow point, allow source-specific post-zero Reaction windows such as Grace Reversal.
9	Resolve post-zero living recovery such as Phoenix Rebirth/Unyielding Light if their target is still alive.
10	Resolve later after-damage triggers/components.
No replay after a death replacement

NEW narrow rule: once a would-die replacement successfully replaces a death result generated by a completed damage packet, do not go back and run Core branches that the packet previously skipped.

So if Ritual of Undeath Death Ward intercepts Core Instant Death:

no Wound save suddenly appears afterward;
no same-packet death-save failure is manufactured;
attached non-death riders may still resolve normally.

Likewise, if it intercepts a fatal Wound save, do not generate another death merely because the same packet had also reduced the creature to 0.

A genuinely separate later effect can still kill it.

C. Damage floors versus HP gain

The initial conclusion stands with one precision:

A damage floor modifies the HP-loss result. It is not HP recovery.

So Redundant Organs, Last King's Gift, Miracle Veil, Song of Mending's floor, Eternal Bloom's floor, Echo Armor, Undying Ember and similar effects can function while Decay says “cannot regain HP.” They do not add HP.

But they do not override Core Instant Death unless the source specifically says they can.

A floor also cannot help someone already at 0 unless its source expressly has a zero-HP/death replacement mode.

D. Ritual Death Ward + Decay needs two snapshots

For cleric.hero.cultist.ritual-of-undeath Death Ward:

Target currently above 0

If the target would die and setting its replacement result to 1 HP is accomplished by reducing HP loss from a positive pre-event HP total, Decay does not interfere. No HP was gained.

Example: packet leaves the creature provisionally at 10 HP but its fourth-Wound failure would kill it. Death Ward replaces death and sets HP to 1. That is a reduction from 10→1, not healing.

Target already at 0

If Death Ward intercepts:

third death-save failure;
zero-HP damage death;
another death event while HP is already 0;

then 0→1 would be HP gain. Decay blocks that gain.

Use the previously discussed NEW Stable-zero bridge, now made deterministic:

Death Ward is consumed, death is prevented, death successes/failures reset, target becomes Stable at 0, immediately roll the normal 1d4 Stable-recovery time and record that absolute deadline.

If Decay still blocks HP recovery when that deadline becomes due, keep one due-blocked recovery event under the reviewed Decay rule. When the block ends, it may resolve if the creature is still living, Stable and at 0.

No indefinite protection exists because the Ward use is gone.

These alter the healing packet before reductions without first requiring an actual HP gain.

Two-pass calculation

Calculate the packet once without restoration-conditioned add-ons.

If that provisional result actually restores at least 1 HP, activate eligible add-ons and recompute the same single healing event from the original amount plus those add-ons.

Do not consume one-shot healing-reduction records twice during that recomputation.

The add-on does not create a new healing event and cannot make itself qualify.

Therefore the requested example is:

base healing 5, applicable flat reduction 5, conditional +3 that requires actual restoration → provisional actual gain is 0 → +3 never triggers → final healing remains 0.

J. Overhealing and Critical Wounds

Define:

ordinaryMissingHP = effectiveMaximumHP - currentHP

After all legal healing bonuses/reductions but before the ordinary max-HP cap:

excess = max(0, postReductionHealingPotential - ordinaryMissingHP)

A broad prohibition such as Decay, Burning-Blood cold-heal rejection, Crownshatter's failed gate, etc. makes excess = 0. Blocked healing cannot be converted.

Critical Wound interaction

Critical's maxHP-1 ceiling does not redefine missing HP.

Example:

effective max 20;
current HP 19;
Critical ceiling =19;
legal post-reduction heal =10.

Ordinary missing HP is still 1.

Therefore:

actual ordinary HP gained =0 because Critical ceiling prevents reaching20;
the missing 1 HP is not excess;
10 - 1 = 9 is genuine amount beyond missing HP and may be eligible for an explicit overheal conversion.
Overflowing Light

If the source heal is otherwise legal, its amount beyond ordinary missing HP may become temp HP up to the printed cap.

Cosmic Equilibrium

It may redirect only genuine excess and only up to its printed S+M/once-round limit.

For each admissible reduction plan:
