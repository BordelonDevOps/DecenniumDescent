# Session zero — agree on the game before play

**Playtest draft: new table procedure awaiting joint review.** Use this conversation before creating the party, and revisit it when a player joins or the campaign changes. Record the agreements below in a place everyone can read. A player can raise a concern privately without explaining personal circumstances.

## 1. Describe the campaign

The Director gives a short public brief: the characters' starting situation, the expedition's shared purpose, the kind of decisions the game will emphasize, and what a typical session involves. Agree on the balance of exploration, tactical combat, social scenes, investigation and downtime. Describe the intended tone in concrete terms, such as adventurous danger, unsettling discovery or desperate survival.

Use the campaign's existing public setting material. Players need a reason to act together and enough information to make choices; they do not need Director secrets or an explanation of the cosmology.

## 2. Set boundaries and a way to pause

Agree which subjects are excluded, which may exist but stay off-screen, and which may be described directly. These agreements can change. A player may ask to pause, skip a description or revise a scene by speaking, sending a message or using another agreed signal. Stop the description, clarify only what is needed, and resume from a point the group accepts. No explanation or character penalty is required for using this table procedure.

Distinguish fictional conflict from permission between players. The proposed default is a cooperative party: attacks, theft, coercion, sabotage or other actions against another player character require the affected player's agreement before resolution. A character feature does not supply that agreement. If the group wants adversarial play, write its specific limits first; anyone affected can decline a particular scene.

## 3. Build characters who can participate

Agree the starting level, available reviewed options and a shared reason to travel together. For a first game, begin with Base-class choices and explain when Arch and Hero stages become available. Use the actual [character creation procedure](../mechanics/CHARACTER_CREATION_PLAYTEST.md), [class schedules](../mechanics/CLASS_PLAYTEST_INDEX.md), [ancestry/background rules](../mechanics/ANCESTRY_BACKGROUND_PLAYTEST.md) and [starting equipment](../mechanics/EQUIPMENT_STARTING_PACKAGES.md).

Each player describes one useful contribution, one connection to another character and one reason their character would support the expedition. Agree how secrets and conflicting loyalties can create interesting choices while leaving everyone able to take part. A character concept that repeatedly prevents cooperation needs revision before play.

## 4. Explain danger and the rules in use

The Director explains the campaign's expected danger, how retreat works in the starting situation, and the current rules for dying, Wounds and recovery. Use the [Director guide](NEWCOMER_DIRECTOR_GUIDE.md) and its linked procedures. Discuss how a player rejoins after character death or retirement; make the replacement plan explicit before it is needed.

Name the current playtest version and any campaign-specific rulings. Teach the [player guide](NEWCOMER_PLAYER_GUIDE.md) and [Resonance reference](RESONANCE_CHEATSHEET.md) using a simple example. Identify options whose operational rules are still pending. A name in an older book or companion menu is not a complete playable effect. Any proposed house rule is recorded separately with its scope and review date.

## 5. Arrange the practical details

Agree session length, breaks, scheduling, notice for absences and the minimum attendance needed to play. Decide who, if anyone, may operate an absent player's character and what decisions require that player's permission. If no one has permission, agree a suitable way for that character to sit out. Record the character's actual location, resources and unresolved effects; attendance arrangements do not themselves award progress, restore resources or change the game's advancement rules.

Choose a shared way to record party inventory, elapsed time and important decisions. Paper is sufficient. If using a companion app, establish which written rule resolves a disagreement between the app and the current playtest text.

## 6. Make participation workable

Ask what formats and pace help people take part. Offer spoken or written declarations, readable references, described map information and breaks. Identify players and conditions with words or symbols as well as color. Let players state a character's intent without performing an accent, voice, gesture or speech. Resolve the character's fictional abilities and limitations under the actual rules, while allowing the player an accessible way to communicate the choice.

Agree how to share spotlight and request thinking time. Before beginning, each person confirms they understand the premise, can participate in the agreed format and knows how to raise a concern. End the first session with a short discussion of what was enjoyable, unclear or difficult, and record the next adjustment.
