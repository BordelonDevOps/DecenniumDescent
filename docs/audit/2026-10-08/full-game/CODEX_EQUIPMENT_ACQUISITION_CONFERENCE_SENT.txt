## Hands, armor and proficiency

One legal AC formula at a time: light base+DEX; medium base+min(DEX,2), including negative DEX; heavy fixed base. One shield supplies its printed bonus only while equipped and usable. Printed Stealth advantage/disadvantage uses ordinary cancellation, including Elven weave. Dwarven plate is AC18, not an additional boost for descriptive 'superior protection'. Descriptive scale-mail slashing protection creates no resistance.

Core expressly replaces V6's prohibition on nonproficient-armored casting: a caster not proficient with worn armor pays +1 RP and has concentration disadvantage. Retain V6's noncasting disadvantage on STR/DEX checks, saves and attacks. PROPOSE: all three shield types require shield proficiency and trigger the same nonproficiency effects while used. RP surcharge applies once per spell regardless of the number of nonproficient pieces; it does not create a pool or an RP price on charge-only ancestry/Oracle casting. Concentration disadvantage still applies to those sources. This price interaction needs your explicit agreement.

PROPOSE missing Strength-column meaning: wearing a listed armor/shield below its STR minimum reduces Speed by 10 feet, once regardless of multiple deficient pieces, floor0. Do not make it a minimum to equip. Do not import a Dwarf exemption absent its exact trait. The reviewed Load procedure remains active; V6 optional encumbrance is an alternate and its penalties do not stack. Ordinary Load Burden and this distinct fitted-equipment penalty can coexist. Confirm that this new completion is preferable to removing the source STR column.

Don/doff retains light1min/1min, medium5min/1min, heavy10min/5min, any shield1Action/1Action. An assisting creature halves doff time only; minimum one actual Action where the source is an Action. No stacking multiple helpers.

Buckler is strapped to one forearm; PROPOSE it can coexist with carrying a small nonweapon object, but does not supply a free S hand, two-handed weapon support, loading hand, active focus manipulation or second weapon attack through that arm. An ordinary shield/tower occupies one hand. Tower supplies only its +3AC, STR13 and Stealth disadvantage, with no invented cover or extra mobility penalty. Soldier Shield Trained is the express exception permitting its actual repeating crossbow with a strapped shield; its reloading requirements and selected-feature eligibility remain intact.

F requires the class's ordinary legal focus, not any impressive object. Preserve V/S and no costly-material waiver. PROPOSE held F and S can use the same hand only if otherwise free of a weapon/shield and manipulating that focus. An ordinary holy symbol may instead be visibly worn or borne on a shield for F, but S still requires its separate usable hand. A two-handed weapon needs both hands only during its attack; releasing one grip costs no action, regripping is part of the next legal use. You cannot do this through occupied hands. Class-card exceptions win.

## Three separate kinds of quality

Preserve V6 equipment grades: Crude armor−1AC/half structuralHP, Fine armor+1AC, Superior+2AC/80%weight, Exceptional+3AC/70%weight; crude weapon−1attack/natural1 causes independent5%break check, Fine+1attack, Superior+2attack/one extra base weapon die on critical, Exceptional+3attack/two extra base weapon dice on critical. No flat damage boost, no automatic magic tag. A critical doubles normal damage dice, then adds these extra quality dice once, not twice. Base weapon die means one die of the printed profile, so greatsword's extra die is d6, not2d6. Crude break check is d100≤5 after an actual natural1 attack; repairable broken object, not erased material.

V6 price multipliers Fine×3, Superior×10, Exceptional×25 apply to ordinary equipment baseline. New Crude price×0.5. Standard×1. Only one equipment grade applies. PROPOSE armor grade modifies worn suits, not shields, because the source does not spell out shield grade stacking; shield quality can affect structuralHP through an explicitly defined crafted output later. Unspecified Exceptional minor special property is no additional effect until an exact property is authored and approved. No free rolled enchantment.

Crafting result labels Standard/Exceptional/Masterwork govern that recipe's yield/charges/catalyst/result rules. They do not automatically change the V6 equipment grade. Forge Dwarf's 'exceptional quality' description remains no free+3AC/attack. A recipe must explicitly specify a resulting equipment grade before it gains one. Core artifact valuation quality×0.25/1/1.5/2.5/5/10 is a third field and applies in its market formula, not as extra ordinary retail markups or extra combat bonuses. Core's artifact valuation and V6 mundane retail rules are scoped, not accidentally multiplied together. Please challenge this separation if source evidence gives a stronger interpretation.

PROPOSE a targeted Sunder is an actual weapon attack against one perceived reachable object/component, consuming one attack if taking the Attack Action; it cannot target hidden components through a creature/body or give a creature free damage too. L6580: Selling Treasure
L6581: Throughout your adventures, you'll discover treasures, equipment, weapons, armor, and other valuable items. When returning to civilization, you can typically sell these findings, provided you can locate interested buyers.
L6582: 
L6583: Standard Items
L6584: As a general rule, undamaged weapons, armor, and other mundane equipment fetch half their cost when sold in a market. Weapons and armor taken from monsters are rarely in good enough condition to sell unless specifically noted by the Director.
L6585: 
L6586: Reputation Impact
L6587: Your reputation with local factions can significantly affect selling prices:
L6588: 
L6589: * Honored: 75% of item value instead of 50%
L6590: * Liked: 60% of item value
L6591: * Neutral: Standard 50% of item value
L6592: * Disliked: 40% of item value
L6593: * Hated: 25% of item value or refusal to trade
L6594: 
L6595: Gems, Jewelry, and Art Objects
L6596: These items retain their full value in the marketplace and can be traded for coin or used directly as currency. For exceptionally valuable treasures, you may need to find a buyer in a major city or trading hub.
L6597: 
L6598: Magic Items
L6599: Selling magic items presents unique challenges. Common consumables like potions or scrolls can usually find buyers in any sizeable settlement, but more powerful or permanent magic items require specialized buyers, typically wealthy collectors, powerful mages, or established factions. The value of magic items transcends simple gold and depends on numerous factors:
L6600: * Item rarity and power
L6601: * Local laws regarding magic
L6602: * Regional demand
L6603: * Your reputation with potential buyers
L6604: * Current political climate
L6605: Unlike mundane equipment, magic items rarely have a fixed market price. Instead, their value is determined through negotiation, often involving services or favors in addition to currency.

L6607: Armor and Shields
L6608: Across Aethel's diverse cultures and technological traditions, armor styles vary dramatically, from the elegant mithril-weave of elven territories to the imposing plate of human kingdoms and the living wood armor of druidic circles. This variety offers adventurers numerous options for protection, each with distinct advantages and limitations. The Armor table collects the most commonly available types of armor found throughout Aethel, separated into three categories: light armor, medium armor, and heavy armor. Many warriors supplement their armor with a shield. 
L6609: Armor Proficiency
L6610: Anyone can put on a suit of armor or strap a shield to an arm, but only those proficient in the armor's use know how to wear it effectively. Your class gives you proficiency with certain types of armor. If you wear armor that you lack proficiency with, you have disadvantage on any ability check, saving throw, or attack roll that involves Strength or Dexterity, and you cannot cast spells.
L6611: 
L6612: Armor Class (AC)
L6613: Armor protects its wearer from attacks. The armor (and shield) you wear determines your base Armor Class.
L6614: 
L6615: Light Armor
L6616: Made from supple and thin materials, light armor favors agile adventurers since it offers some protection without sacrificing mobility. If you wear light armor, you add your Dexterity modifier to the base number from your armor type to determine your Armor Class.
L6617: 
L6618: Padded. Consisting of quilted layers of cloth and batting, padded armor provides basic protection while allowing freedom of movement. Common among novice adventurers and those in regions where metals are scarce.
L6619: 
L6620: Leather. This armor features a breastplate and shoulder protectors crafted from leather that has been hardened by boiling in oil, while the remainder consists of softer, more flexible materials. Standard among scouts, thieves, and those who value stealth.
L6621: 
L6622: Studded Leather. Made from tough but flexible leather reinforced with close-set rivets or spikes, studded leather offers improved protection while maintaining mobility. Favored by rangers, rogues, and duelists.
L6623: 
L6624: Elven Weave. A rare armor crafted from specially treated silk interwoven with enchanted threads, elven weave is lightweight and whisper-quiet. Primarily found in elven territories, it offers superior protection for its weight.
L6625: 
L6626: Medium Armor
L6627: 
L6628: Medium armor offers more protection than light armor but impairs movement more significantly. If you wear medium armor, you add your Dexterity modifier, to a maximum of +2, to the base number from your armor type to determine your Armor Class.
L6629: 
L6630: Hide. Consisting of thick furs and pelts, hide armor provides protection against both weapons and harsh elements. Common among tribal warriors, barbarians, and frontier settlers.
L6631: 
L6632: Chain Shirt. Made of interlocking metal rings, a chain shirt is worn between layers of clothing or leather. It offers modest protection to the wearer's upper body while allowing the sound of rings to be muffled by outer layers.
L6633: 
L6634: Scale Mail. This armor consists of a coat and leggings (and sometimes a separate skirt) of leather covered with overlapping pieces of metal, similar to fish scales. The suit includes gauntlets and offers good protection against slashing attacks.
L6635: 
L6636: Breastplate. Consisting of a fitted metal chest piece worn with supple leather, a breastplate protects vital organs while leaving the limbs relatively unencumbered. Popular among commanders and wealthy adventurers who value mobility.
L6637: 
L6638: Half Plate. Half plate consists of shaped metal plates that cover most of the wearer's body except for the legs, which are protected by simple greaves attached with leather straps. It represents a compromise between protection and mobility.
L6639: 
L6640: Heavy Armor
L6641: 
L6642: Of all armor categories, heavy armor offers the best protection. These suits cover the entire body and are designed to stop a wide range of attacks, but only proficient warriors can manage their weight and bulk. Heavy armor doesn't let you add your Dexterity modifier to your Armor Class, but it also doesn't penalize you if your Dexterity modifier is negative.
L6643: 
L6644: Ring Mail. This armor consists of leather armor with heavy rings sewn into it. The rings reinforce the armor against blows from swords and axes. Ring mail is typically worn by those who cannot afford better armor.
L6645: 
L6646: Chain Mail. Made of interlocking metal rings, chain mail includes a layer of quilted fabric worn underneath to prevent chafing and cushion impact. The suit includes gauntlets and offers good all-around protection.
L6647: 
L6648: Splint. Splint armor is made of narrow vertical strips of metal riveted to a backing of leather worn over cloth padding. Flexible chain mail protects the joints. It offers better protection than chain mail but less than plate.
L6649: 
L6650: Plate. Plate consists of shaped, interlocking metal plates that cover the entire body. A suit includes gauntlets, heavy leather boots, a visored helmet, and thick padding underneath. Buckles and straps distribute the weight across the body. It represents the pinnacle of non-magical protection.
L6651: 
L6652: Dwarven Plate. Masterwork armor forged by dwarven smiths, dwarven plate is lighter and more flexible than standard plate while offering superior protection. Its creation requires rare metals and techniques known only to dwarven master crafters.

L6680: Shields
L6681: 
L6682: Shields are made from wood, metal, or exotic materials and are carried in one hand. Wielding a shield increases your Armor Class by the indicated amount. You can benefit from only one shield at a time.
L6683: 
L6684: Buckler. A small shield strapped to the forearm, leaving the hand partially free. It provides minimal protection but allows for greater mobility.
L6685:

L6687: 
L6688: Tower Shield. A massive shield that can cover most of the body. While offering superior protection, it significantly reduces mobility and requires considerable strength to wield effectively.

L6705: Armor Quality Tiers
L6706: 
L6707: Beyond the standard armors listed above, Aethel's master crafters can create equipment of exceptional quality:
L6708: 
L6709: Crude (-1). Hastily made or damaged armor that provides less protection than standard versions. AC is reduced by 1, and the armor has half the normal hit points.
L6710: 
L6711: Standard (0). The baseline quality represented in the Armor Table.
L6712: 
L6713: Fine (+1). Expertly crafted armor that provides better protection without additional weight. AC is increased by 1.
L6714: 
L6715: Superior (+2). Masterwork armor created by renowned artisans using premium materials. AC is increased by 2, and the armor's weight is reduced by 20%.
L6716: 
L6717: Exceptional (+3). Legendary armor of the highest craftsmanship, often incorporating rare materials. AC is increased by 3, weight is reduced by 30%, and the armor gains one minor special property.
L6718: 
L6719: Higher quality tiers typically cost significantly more than standard versions:
L6720: * Fine: 3× standard cost
L6721: * Superior: 10× standard cost
L6722: * Exceptional: 25× standard cost

L6724: Weapons
L6725: 
L6726: Your choice of weapon reflects not only your combat style but often your cultural background, training, and personal philosophy. From the elegant elven thinblade to the devastating dwarven warhammer, weapons in Aethel are as diverse as its inhabitants.
L6727: 
L6728: Weapon Proficiency
L6729: 
L6730: Your race, class, and feats can grant you proficiency with certain weapons or categories of weapons. The two main categories are simple and martial. Most people can use simple weapons with proficiency. These weapons include clubs, maces, and other weapons often found in the hands of commoners. Martial weapons, including swords, axes, and polearms, require more specialized training to use effectively. Most warriors use martial weapons because these weapons put their fighting style and training to best use.
L6731: 
L6732: Proficiency with a weapon allows you to add your proficiency bonus to the attack roll for any attack you make with that weapon. If you make an attack roll using a weapon with which you lack proficiency, you do not add your proficiency bonus to the attack roll.
L6733: 
L6734: Weapon Properties
L6735: 
L6736: Many weapons have special properties related to their use, as shown in the Weapons table.
L6737: 
L6738: Ammunition. You can use a weapon that has the ammunition property to make a ranged attack only if you have ammunition to fire from the weapon. Each time you attack with the weapon, you expend one piece of ammunition. Drawing the ammunition from a quiver, case, or other container is part of the attack. At the end of the battle, you can recover half your expended ammunition by taking a minute to search the battlefield.
L6739: 
L6740: If you use a weapon that has the ammunition property to make a melee attack, you treat the weapon as an improvised weapon. A sling must be loaded to deal any damage when used in this way.
L6741: 
L6742: Finesse. When making an attack with a finesse weapon, you use your choice of your Strength or Dexterity modifier for the attack and damage rolls. You must use the same modifier for both rolls.
L6743: 
L6744: Heavy. Small creatures have disadvantage on attack rolls with heavy weapons. A heavy weapon's size and bulk make it too large for a Small creature to use effectively.
L6745: 
L6746: Light. A light weapon is small and easy to handle, making it ideal for use when fighting with two weapons.
L6747: 
L6748: Loading. Because of the time required to load this weapon, you can fire only one piece of ammunition from it when you use an action, bonus action, or reaction to fire it, regardless of the number of attacks you can normally make.
L6749: 
L6750: Range. A weapon that can be used to make a ranged attack has a range shown in parentheses after the ammunition or thrown property. The range lists two numbers. The first is the weapon's normal range in feet, and the second indicates the weapon's maximum range. When attacking a target beyond normal range, you have disadvantage on the attack roll. You can't attack a target beyond the weapon's long range.
L6751: 
L6752: Reach. This weapon adds 5 feet to your reach when you attack with it.
L6753: 
L6754: Special. A weapon with the special property has unusual rules governing its use, explained in the weapon's description.
L6755: 
L6756: Thrown. If a weapon has the thrown property, you can throw the weapon to make a ranged attack. If the weapon is a melee weapon, you use the same ability modifier for that attack roll and damage roll that you would use for a melee attack with the weapon. For example, if you throw a handaxe, you use your Strength, but if you throw a dagger, you can use either your Strength or your Dexterity, since the dagger has the finesse property.
L6757: 
L6758: Two-Handed. This weapon requires two hands to use.
L6759: 
L6760: Versatile. This weapon can be used with one or two hands. A damage value in parentheses appears with the property, the damage when the weapon is used with two hands to make a melee attack.

L6820: Special Weapons
L6821: 
L6822: Lance. You have disadvantage when you use a lance to attack a target within 5 feet of you. Also, a lance requires two hands to wield when you aren't mounted.
L6823: 
L6824: Net. A Large or smaller creature hit by a net is restrained until it is freed. A net has no effect on creatures that are formless, or creatures that are Huge or larger. A creature can use its action to make a DC 10 Strength check, freeing itself or another creature within its reach on a success. Dealing 5 slashing damage to the net (AC 10) also frees the creature without harming it, ending the effect and destroying the net. When you use an action, bonus action, or reaction to attack with a net, you can make only one attack regardless of the number of attacks you can normally make.
L6825: 
L6826: Improvised Weapons
L6827: 
L6828: Sometimes characters don't have their weapons and have to attack with whatever is close at hand. An improvised weapon includes any object you can wield in one or two hands, such as broken glass, a table leg, a frying pan, a wagon wheel, or a dead goblin.
L6829: 
L6830: In many cases, an improvised weapon is similar to an actual weapon and can be treated as such. For example, a table leg is akin to a club. At the Director's option, a character proficient with a weapon can use a similar object as if it were that weapon and use their proficiency bonus.
L6831: 
L6832: An object that bears no resemblance to a weapon deals 1d4 damage (the Director assigns a damage type appropriate to the object). If a character uses a ranged weapon to make a melee attack, or throws a melee weapon that does not have the thrown property, it also deals 1d4 damage. An improvised thrown weapon has a normal range of 20 feet and a long range of 60 feet.
L6833: 
L6834: Silvered Weapons
L6835: 
L6836: Some monsters that have immunity or resistance to nonmagical weapons are susceptible to silver weapons, so cautious adventurers invest extra coin to plate their weapons with silver. You can silver a single weapon or ten pieces of ammunition for 100 gp. This cost represents not only the price of the silver, but the time and expertise needed to add silver to the weapon without making it less effective.
L6837: 
L6838: Weapon Quality Tiers
L6839: 
L6840: Like armor, weapons in Aethel can be crafted to different quality standards:
L6841: 
L6842: Crude (-1). Poorly made or damaged weapons that are less effective in combat. Attack rolls suffer a -1 penalty, and the weapon has a 5% chance of breaking on a natural 1.
L6843: 
L6844: Standard (0). The baseline quality represented in the Weapons Table.
L6845: 
L6846: Fine (+1). Expertly crafted weapons that provide better accuracy. Attack rolls gain a +1 bonus.
L6847: 
L6848: Superior (+2). Masterwork weapons created by renowned weaponsmiths using premium materials. Attack rolls gain a +2 bonus, and critical hits deal one additional die of damage.
L6849: 
L6850: Exceptional (+3). Legendary weapons of the highest craftsmanship, often incorporating rare materials. Attack rolls gain a +3 bonus, critical hits deal two additional dice of damage, and the weapon gains one minor special property.
L6851: 
L6852: Higher quality tiers typically cost significantly more than standard versions:
L6853: * Fine: 3× standard cost
L6854: * Superior: 10× standard cost
L6855: * Exceptional: 25× standard cost

L7867: Lifting and Carrying
L7868: 
L7869: Your Strength score determines the amount of weight you can bear. The following terms define what you can lift or carry.
L7870: 
L7871: Carrying Capacity. Your carrying capacity is your Strength score multiplied by 15. This is the weight (in pounds) that you can carry, which is high enough that most characters don't usually have to worry about it.
L7872: 
L7873: Push, Drag, or Lift. You can push, drag, or lift a weight in pounds up to twice your carrying capacity (or 30 times your Strength score). While pushing or dragging weight in excess of your carrying capacity, your speed drops to 5 feet.
L7874: 
L7875: Size and Strength. Larger creatures can bear more weight, whereas Tiny creatures can carry less. For each size category above Medium, double the creature's carrying capacity and the amount it can push, drag, or lift. For a Tiny creature, halve these weights.
L7876: 
L7877: Variant: Encumbrance
L7878: 
L7879: The rules for lifting and carrying are intentionally simple. Here is a variant if you are looking for more detailed rules for determining how a character is hindered by the weight of equipment. When you use this variant, ignore the Strength column of the Armor table in Chapter 5.
L7880: 
L7881: If you carry weight in excess of 5 times your Strength score, you are encumbered, which means your speed drops by 10 feet.
L7882: 
L7883: If you carry weight in excess of 10 times your Strength score, up to your maximum carrying capacity, you are instead heavily encumbered, which means your speed drops by 20 feet and you have disadvantage on ability checks, attack rolls, and saving throws that use Strength, Dexterity, or Constitution.

A named weapon, kit, relic, focus, kama, nunchaku or other implement still requires an actual equipment record for its statistics and use. Proficiency does not grant ownership or create an item profile.

Bard instruments use the ordinary improvised-weapon procedure without an invented die or extra attack. The Cleric favored weapon is one already-existing mundane record chosen and recorded at creation, with no free later change. Ranger’s dagger/shortsword/scimitar definition is a new bounded interpretation of “dual-wielded blades”; it does not remove normal wielding requirements or grant a second attack.

Soldier sidearms must be explicitly classified in actual equipment records; the equipment pass still has to supply those bindings. Warrior mutation-tagged gear is a bonus-eligibility hook, not a proficiency category. Mage/Wizard Wand, Staff, Arcane Staff, Spell Focus and Spellblade source labels grant no weapon statistics or magical powers by themselves. Druid retains its nonmetal restriction. Monk’s Unarmored Flow follows its own card.

A legitimate tool check adds proficiency once if trained; skill plus tool training does not add PB twice. Arcana tools, religious implements, divination tools and survival tools remain source-preserved proficiency labels, with actual equipment procedures still required.

They are not merged back into first-class starting lists. Paladin’s starting Martial Proficiency card cannot bypass the multiclass table to grant heavy armor.

# Bodies equipment and carrying

**REVIEWED — authored tabletop body, equipment, carrying and continuous meld procedures.** New integration mechanics remain explicitly provisional; runtime implementation is pending. These procedures connect the [Druid form rules](FORM_TEMP_HP_PLAYTEST.md) and [transformation spells](TRANSFORMATION_SPELL_PLAYTEST.md). They apply to the same character, HP record and physical belongings throughout a change. They grant no additional forms, spell access, actions or resources. The approved books remain preserved.

## Entering and changing forms

Before paying for a voluntary change, choose a legal form and size, check its space at your current position, and identify any equipment that must be dropped. If the destination or required drops are illegal, the change does not begin and spends nothing. Otherwise pay the source's normal costs and resolve its actual entry effects once. A successful hostile or forced transformation cannot be prevented merely by excess gear: resolve it under its source rules, then reconcile excess equipment by keeping it external, dropping it or recording pending placement. Other source requirements still apply.

Your Druid form uses your own HP, ability scores, saves, proficiency and conditions. It supplies only its printed anatomy and features. Speech does not imply hands: none of the 26 current Druid profiles grants ordinary hand-based equipment use. Of the 12 spell profiles, only Humanoid grants hands. Each profile retains its exact size and movement permissions.

| Change | Equipment procedure |
|---|---|
| Root body to first Druid form | Record and meld the equipment physically worn, held or carried by your body, including carried containers and their contents. |
| Druid form to another Druid form | Keep the same original melded equipment. End the former form's dependent effects and start the new form under its normal rules. Reconcile newly acquired external items with the new anatomy. |
| A successful spell replaces a Druid or spell form | Close the former transformation and capture the legitimate resulting gear under the new spell's meld rules in one continuous change. There is no moment when you can use the returning equipment. |
| Shapechange changes its current spell profile | Keep compatible external items. Drop incompatible external items; they do not join the original melded equipment. |
| Suppression | Follow the spell's suppression procedure. It is not an ending or a fresh capture of equipment. Original melded gear stays inaccessible. |
| Final reversion, expiry or forced ending | Return to root anatomy, end the source's dependent effects and reconcile surviving equipment as described below. |

The continuing meld record across Druid-to-Druid changes is a **new provisional integration rule**. It prevents shifting from opening an equipment-use window between forms. A spell replacement retains the already-reviewed close-and-capture procedure.

## What melds

Record where each item was before the first change: worn in a particular slot, held in a particular hand, or carried in a particular container or on the body. Ownership alone is insufficient. A sword stored in a wagon stays in the wagon when its owner becomes a Hawk. A legitimately borrowed sword in your actual custody can meld and return normally; the lender remains its owner. Changing ownership metadata does not itself move an inaccessible physical item.

A backpack and each of its contents remain distinct items. Count each item's own weight or Load once; a container does not erase its contents. Melded items remain in your custody but are inaccessible. You cannot withdraw or add contents, use components, consume supplies, fire ammunition or activate equipment through the meld.

Melding never repairs damage, reloads a weapon, restores charges, replenishes supplies or creates a replacement item. Destruction, consumption and legitimate transfers remain real. Keep the current condition and quantity of each actual item.

## Using equipment in a form

| Item or effect | Rule |
|---|---|
| Original armor and shield | Melded and unusable. Exclude their worn or held AC benefits from the form's normal AC. |
| Held weapon, tool, focus or material component | Requires actual access and the appropriate anatomy. A form without hands receives no general permission to use it. |
| Effect requiring an item to be worn, held or equipped | Suspended while the item is melded. |
| Effect requiring only possession or attunement | May continue only when its exact rule is independent of the lost anatomy and physical use. An unspecified magic bonus is not automatically operative. |
| Newly acquired armor, harness, collar or container | Must fit the actual size and anatomy and have a legal way to remain attached or carried. Hands are required for held items, not automatically for every wearable item. |
| Newly acquired external gear when the form changes | Remains external if compatible. Otherwise use the normal drop procedure. It does not silently join an existing meld record. |

The possession-versus-use distinction and individual compatibility checks are **new provisional defaults**. A specific item or form can supply an explicit exception. Do not infer one from a creature's name, appearance or ability to speak. Item-specific compatibility across the full equipment catalog remains to be authored.

## Returning equipment

Resolve a final reversion in this order:

1. Keep current external items that still exist and remain legally equipped or carried by the root body.
2. Return each surviving melded item that remains in the character's legitimate custody to its recorded former slot or container, if available and legal.
3. If an external item now occupies that slot, leave it there. Return the former item as carried inventory if there is a legal place and sufficient capacity.
4. Drop an item that cannot be carried or equipped at the current position or the nearest legal adjacent item position.
5. If no legal drop position exists, record that item's placement as pending. It continues to exist; it is not duplicated, destroyed or used to displace another creature.

Current external equipment taking priority over a former slot is a **new provisional integration rule**. A returned sword may go into available carried storage instead of replacing a sword you actually acquired and equipped during the transformation. Both retain their current condition and ownership.

Destroyed, consumed, physically transferred, lost or otherwise unavailable items never reappear. An item in another custodian's possession cannot return merely because you still claim ownership of it. Someone else's legal ownership does not prevent an intact borrowed item from returning to your continuing legitimate custody.

## Placement and involuntary endings

A transformation does not grant movement. A voluntary size change keeps the same spatial anchor and must fit before payment. Preserve the existing footprint on a same-size change when possible.

A mandatory ending still occurs if the root anatomy does not fit. First try its normal space at the same position, then a legal squeeze under the [movement rules](COMBAT_ACTION_PLAYTEST.md). If neither fits, record a placement conflict. The **new provisional placement procedure** permits only a nearest legal adjacent placement in the same unobstructed region. It cannot pass through a wall, closed barrier or occupied space. Choose the minimum displacement that fits the whole body; among ties, preserve the greatest overlap with its previous footprint. If still tied, the Director uses a fixed neutral board order, not a tactical choice by the affected player. If no adjacent candidate fits, the conflict remains until the obstruction is removed or a legal relocation effect resolves it. Do not search farther afield.

This mandatory normalization spends no movement and triggers no opportunity attack, charge distance, movement benefit or other movement rider. It changes no remaining movement allowance and grants no invented crushing damage. It does not permit a voluntary form change to bypass its preflight.

Track actual height and terrain. Losing flight while unsupported starts ordinary falling from the current location; reversion never moves the creature directly to the ground. Ending a burrowing form underground does not move the creature to the surface. Master of Vermin's permission to pass through Tiny gaps changes clearance, not its printed size.

Use the actual space of the expressed anatomy:

| Size | Space | On a grid of 2.5-foot subcells |
|---|---|---|
| Tiny | 2.5 by 2.5 feet | 1 subcell |
| Small | 5 by 5 feet | 2 by 2 subcells |
| Medium | 5 by 5 feet | 2 by 2 subcells |
| Large | 10 by 10 feet | 4 by 4 subcells |

V6 explicitly supplies Tiny and Medium space. Small and Large complete this bounded table provisionally; the supplied V6 passage does not separately list them. Size category still matters even when two sizes occupy the same space. Squeezing changes clearance and its penalties; it does not change size, carrying capacity or weight. Exact Tiny occupancy requires subcells or equivalent measurement, not treating Tiny as Medium.

## Body weight and conditions

**New provisional rule:** a creature-form transformation preserves the body's existing base physical weight unless an exact rule changes it. Size alone does not change weight. A 180-pound character therefore remains 180 pounds as a Tiny Hawk or Large Bear. This is a magical transformation rule, not a claim about the weight of ordinary animals. It applies equally to the current 26 Druid and 12 creature-form spell profiles.

Record base body weight separately from equipment and condition effects. Petrified multiplies current body weight by ten once; it does not rewrite the base weight or multiply repeatedly for multiple applications. Ending or changing a form does not cure that condition. Temporary Druid form ends under its existing Incapacitated rule, then Petrified continues to apply to the reverted body. An anatomy permitted to persist keeps its own base weight and the same condition overlay.

True Polymorph object anatomy uses its actual documented output weight. Its output ceiling remains the lower of current effective input weight and earliest recorded origin weight. Include a current applicable weight-changing condition in the input, but never rebase the origin ceiling upward. Equipment, casualty Load and estimated pounds do not establish material available for a transformation. Creature-origin object anatomy has no independent sale, salvage or harvest value.

For another creature, mount, floor or platform supporting you, count your effective body weight plus all actual equipment in your custody, including melded items, once. For your own inventory capacity, count what you carry, not your own body weight. Conditions change only the items or body their exact rules affect.

## Carrying and Load

Use pounds for actual weight and Load for inventory burden. Extraction Class describes cargo danger and attention. A casualty's Load describes the difficulty of transporting a body; it is not the body's weight. These quantities answer different questions and never convert automatically into each other.

The following is a **new provisional reconciliation** of V6 physical handling with the staged Gameplay Loop inventory rules. It does not promote the historical Gameplay Loop file into the approved books. V6's optional encumbrance variant remains preserved as an alternative; do not add its penalties to this Load procedure.

| Carrier size | Physical carry limit in pounds | Push, drag or lift limit in pounds | Inventory capacity in Load | Casualty Load for a body of this size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tiny | Strength score × 7.5 | Strength score × 15 | Strength score ÷ 2 | 1.5 |
| Small | Strength score × 15 | Strength score × 30 | Maximum of 0 and Strength score − 2 | 3 |
| Medium | Strength score × 15 | Strength score × 30 | Strength score | 6 |
| Large | Strength score × 30 | Strength score × 60 | Strength score × 2 | 12 |

Use the character's current Strength score, not the modifier or an imported monster score. Tiny's Inventory capacity and casualty Load are new provisional additions. The pound limits come from V6; Small, Medium and Large Load values come from the Gameplay Loop. Larger unsupported transport sizes require their own completed rule before this table can handle them.

Keep fractional Loads without rounding. An item with a printed Load uses that value. If it has only a known weight, its **provisional Load is pounds divided by ten**. A Load-only item may use ten pounds per Load as a clearly labeled estimate for ordinary hauling; this is insufficient for effects requiring documented physical mass.

Count a container's own empty Load plus the contents' Loads, unless an explicitly inclusive Load already covers those exact contents. Handle pounds separately: sum the empty container weight and contents' weights, or use an explicitly inclusive gross weight for the contents it covers. An inclusive Load does not make a printed weight inclusive, or vice versa. Apply this rule through nested containers and meld budgets so every item counts exactly once in each quantity.

With positive carried Load at or above half capacity and no greater than capacity, you are **Burdened**: reduce Speed by 10 feet and apply disadvantage to checks to climb, swim or move silently. An empty inventory is not Burdened, including at zero capacity. Above capacity, that inventory cannot be carried routinely; drop or transfer something, or use a legal alternative such as dragging or a vehicle. These threshold and zero-Load details are provisional clarifications.

Apply actual physical limits when handling a known weight. Between physical carry capacity and twice that capacity, pushing or dragging uses the lower of otherwise-current Speed and 5 feet. A Speed of 0 stays 0; a Speed of 3 stays 3. Lifting does not independently permit movement: moving while supporting a weight must satisfy carrying, pushing, dragging or another exact rule. An anatomy restriction, such as Hawk's prohibition on carrying a Medium ally, still applies even if numbers would fit. Burden penalties do not create hands, harnesses, lift or access to melded gear.

Transporting a creature must pass both checks: the carrier can physically handle the body's effective weight and associated equipment, and can accommodate the casualty Load plus the equipment's Load. Petrified multiplies the body weight by ten, not its casualty Load. Removing gear can reduce equipment weight and Load; it cannot reduce the body's weight or erase its condition.

## Meld limits across repeated transformations

**New provisional meld exception:** legally melded gear is excluded from both your transformed body's active Inventory Load and its own active pound-carrying limit. Record its full Load and weight separately while it remains inaccessible. New external equipment uses the expressed form's current capacities and still needs compatible anatomy and custody.

A **continuous meld chain** begins when root anatomy first enters a transformation that melds gear. At that instant, record two fixed ceilings: the pre-transformation body's ordinary active Load capacity and its ordinary physical carrying capacity in pounds. Legally melded gear must fit both. Keep the actual total Load and pounds of the hidden gear, counting containers and contents once in each quantity.

Druid-to-Druid changes, internal Shapechange switches, suppression, permanent True Polymorph and continuous replacement by another spell do not end the chain or increase its ceilings. Later changes to size or Strength neither increase nor retroactively shrink these historical ceilings. An existing item is not expelled merely to make room for a new one.

A later spell replacement may capture an external item only if the whole item fits the remaining room under **both** ceilings. Do not partially meld an item unless its source defines a divisible cargo quantity. A new item that will not fit remains external if compatible with the destination and within its current carrying limits; otherwise drop it or, for a forced resolution without legal placement, record its placement as pending. Excess cargo never grants immunity to a hostile or forced transformation. Druid-to-Druid changes continue to preserve their existing meld and never recapture external cargo.

For a voluntary change, declare and preflight the full item disposition before spending costs. A successful hostile or forced change resolves its mandatory custody consequences without deleting items or refunding its costs. This same overflow procedure applies at the first forced transformation if the target began with excess cargo; only gear within both starting ceilings can become hidden.

When someone else carries the transformed character, count its casualty Load plus both its melded and external equipment Load. Count the effective physical body weight plus all that equipment for actual weight handling. Melding therefore provides no weightless cargo for another carrier.

Only an actual final return to root anatomy, with the hidden equipment released and reconciled, ends the chain. Returned gear rejoins active inventory under the root body's **current** capacities. Resolve excess with the return-and-drop procedure; overload cannot prevent a mandatory reversion. A later new transformation can then establish a new pair of ceilings from its actual legal starting state.

## Extraction Class and Secure Cargo

Extraction Class is a danger and logistics rating, not a carrying allowance:

| Class | Cargo |
|---|---|
| 0 | Ordinary trade goods |
| 1 | Common monster parts or durable salvage |
| 2 | Uncommon, fragile or regulated cargo |
| 3 | Rare artifacts, volatile substances or living captives |
| 4 | Very rare strategic cargo or dangerous curses |
| 5 | Epic cargo likely to attract sovereign or faction intervention |
| 6 | Legendary cargo requiring a dedicated extraction operation |

Retain relevant Fragile, Volatile, Cursed, Living, Contraband, Perishable and Attention tags. A light object can carry a high Extraction Class, and a heavy ordinary crate can remain Class 0.

**New provisional wording for Secure Cargo:** spend an Action on one legal attach, lift, transfer or protective operation involving one discrete cargo unit or bundle with an assigned Extraction Class. The unit must be physically handleable as declared; naming an entire convoy a bundle does not make it one operation. The action grants no extra capacity, hands, equipment, automatic success, invulnerability or reduction in Extraction Class. Apply actual restraints, harnesses, checks, hazards and source-specific handling requirements.

## Examples

**The remote sword.** A Druid wears armor, holds a staff and carries a pack containing two potions. A spare sword is in a wagon. The armor, staff, pack and both potions meld when the Druid changes. The sword remains in the wagon. Neither potion can be consumed from the melded pack.

**A compatible harness.** A transformed creature acquires a harness made for its present anatomy. A later change checks the harness against the destination form. If it does not fit, the voluntary change requires a legal drop. The harness is neither erased nor added to the old melded equipment.

**The crowded hand.** A spell Humanoid acquires and holds a new weapon. When the spell ends, root anatomy can keep that weapon if legal. The original melded weapon returns to an available legal carrying position, or is dropped if necessary. It does not replace or duplicate the new weapon.

**The loaded Hawk.** A Strength 9 Druid becomes Tiny with 3 Load of legally melded gear. It stays inaccessible and uses neither of the Hawk's active carrying capacities. Tiny capacity is 4.5 Load and 67.5 pounds. An additional package of 3 Load and 30 pounds in a genuinely compatible external carrying arrangement fits both capacities, but exceeds half the Load capacity of 2.25 and makes the Druid Burdened. A rescuer carrying that Hawk counts 1.5 casualty Load plus all 6 Load of equipment, totaling 7.5 Load, and separately satisfies the full body and equipment weight.

**The repeated replacement.** A Strength 9 Medium character starts a chain with 8 Load and 80 pounds of legal gear. The fixed ceilings are 9 Load and 135 pounds. The remaining hidden capacity is therefore 1 Load and 55 pounds. A later spell cannot hide a new 3-Load, 30-pound package even if the current form can carry it externally. A whole 0.5-Load, 50-pound item fits both remaining ceilings; a 0.5-Load, 60-pound item fails the pound limit. Changing form or casting again does not refresh either ceiling.

**The stone casualty.** A Medium ally has a documented base weight of 180 pounds and becomes Petrified. The body now weighs 1,800 pounds but still contributes 6 casualty Load. A Strength 18 Medium rescuer can normally carry 270 pounds or push, drag or lift 540 pounds, so cannot move that body alone through these ordinary procedures, even with enough spare Load capacity. Additional gear is counted separately.

## Encounter and action resources

Establish terrain, light, hazards, objectives, exits and cargo; determine awareness; roll `d20 + DEX modifier + legal bonuses`. Highest first. Players choose player/player ties; the Director resolves ties involving NPCs. Keep the resulting ordered slots for the encounter. A round represents approximately six seconds. A **regular turn** is a creature's normal initiative slot even if it cannot act. An extra turn is distinct.

A turn normally permits movement, one Action, a Bonus Action when an actual rule grants a use, and one ordinary free object interaction. A second ordinary interaction or an Action-time object activation uses an Action. Brief communication is not an extra Action. Conditions and specific features may restrict these permissions.

The shared limit is one **extra full Action per creature per numbered round**. Such an Action cannot grant another extra Action, Bonus Action or Reaction. This cap is independent of Reaction timing. Extra turns do not by themselves refresh Reactions, recharge, periodic ticks, recovery ticks or the regular-turn allowances defined here.

**Reaction interpretation.** Begin an encounter with one available Reaction unless surprised or otherwise prohibited. Refresh at each regular own-turn start; never at the numbered round boundary or an extra turn. A late-initiative creature can therefore react before its first turn, refresh, and react again in the same numbered round. A legal trigger, available resource and all feature prerequisites are still required.

**Explicit additional Reactions.** A reviewed card can grant a separate limited use without refreshing the ordinary resource. Warrior Mind Shock supplies its stated once-per-duration non-attack Reaction; spending one resource does not consume/reset the other. Surprise, Incapacitated and Focused still restrict both. It grants no Counterspell knowledge or waived costs. Extra turns refresh neither. Per-dose reapplication/stacking and other specific grants remain part of the class semantic pass.

**Surprise — V6 plus NEW Bonus Action clarification.** On a surprised creature's first regular turn it cannot voluntarily move or take an Action or Bonus Action. It cannot react until that turn ends. Its slot, durations, passive effects, concentration, periodic effects and recharge still occur. Surprise alone is not Incapacitated or Unconscious. Brief communication and an otherwise legal simple free interaction remain possible.

**Entry — NEW.** Standard end-round reinforcements roll/receive initiative and first act next round. An explicit midround entrant acts this round only if its complete ordered slot is still upcoming; entry during that exact slot counts as already passed. It has its initial Reaction unless prohibited. A summon-specific timing rule overrides this generic entry procedure.

After the final slot, resolve Core's end-round hazards, reinforcements, morale, objectives and boss phases. Conclude the scene with surrender, capture, pursuit, harvesting access and noise consequences under their own rules; winning initiative does not resolve those automatically.

## Time and event ordering — NEW

For a duration created in initiative, record the relevant original regular start/end marker. Existing effects keep that **virtual marker**, repeating every six seconds if the creature leaves or changes initiative. Reordering, rerolling, extra turns, death/removal or leaving/reentering initiative never restart an existing duration. New effects use the current schedule. Source death still ends concentration, a source-dependent aura/tether or an effect explicitly ending on death.

- A bare hostile target-bound **N rounds** ends at the Nth future regular target-end marker. An in-progress target turn's current end does not count.
- A bare self/source buff, ally-targeted buff or source-controlled aura **N rounds** ends at the Nth future source-start marker. Explicit target-turn, pulse or absolute timing overrides this default. Activation during source turn 1 for three rounds ends at source turn 4's start.
- **Next turn** means the next future regular turn of the named creature, not the current turn's still-upcoming end. An unqualified next turn refers to source/user under the existing shared rule. Explicit “this turn” can include the current turn.
- An outside-initiative bare N-round effect instead gets an absolute application-time + N×6 seconds deadline. Entering initiative preserves that deadline.
- Explicit periodic profiles retain their registered clocks and tick counts. Never create a second timer merely because a generic duration rule also exists.

The [exact GPT review](../../../../docs/audit/2026-10-08/full-game/GPT_COMBAT_EXACT_FINDINGS.md) confirms ally-buff source clocks and excluding the current turn for explicit next-turn endpoints. The helper excludes a specific current-end marker only if that marker would otherwise be selected: application during a turn at time1 with end3 excludes3 and selects9; application at/after3 also selects9, without erroneously skipping again to15.

At a regular turn's **start**, resolve in this order:

1. Expire start-ending effects and apply immediate state changes from those expirations.
2. Refresh Reaction; tick regular-start recharge/non-action clocks.
3. Resolve already-due absolute state recovery if now legal, including a previously blocked Stable recovery deadline.
4. If still alive, dying and at 0 HP with death saves, make that turn's death save.
5. Resolve other start effects and periodic events in application order.
6. Begin voluntary turn decisions.

At **end**, resolve triggered effects, due damage, saves and maintenance before end-ending effects expire; then the slot finishes. No extra turn creates another regular marker. The caller must not reuse a resolved marker.

For simultaneous effects, earlier application timestamp precedes later. For simultaneous components of one source, its printed internal order controls; otherwise use stable application sequence, with source/mechanical ID as a final tie-break. Record that sequence when creating the effects. Beneficial/harmful labels do not change priority. Periodic family arbitration happens once per family/marker before its damage, retaining the family registry's consumed loser ticks.

Examples: dying at 0 rolls its death save before ordinary start-turn healing; a fatal death save is not undone by ordinary healing. Stable at 0 skips the save, then start-turn damage can break Stable. A natural 20 can restore 1 HP before a due Bleed drops it to 0 as a new drop event. If Decay expires while a Stable recovery deadline is already due, the old deadline resolves once without rerolling recovery time.

## Standard Actions

| Action | Procedure |

| Attack | Make the attacks legally granted by class and weapon. Replacing one attack does not replace the entire Action unless specified. |
| Cast | Use an Action-time spell or Resonance technique; all known/prepared, cost, component and target requirements apply. |
| Dash | Add one current Speed to each mode's turn cap; see movement ledger. Speed 0 grants none. A second legally paid Dash adds another Speed. |
| Disengage | Voluntary movement does not provoke opportunity attacks for the rest of that turn. |

| Guard | +2 AC against the first attack against you before next regular start; consumed on that attack hit or miss. Guard state and Interpose permission remain until expiry even after the +2 is spent. |
| Aim | Action; no prior voluntary movement this turn, and no subsequent voluntary movement that turn. +2 to one ranged/spell attack before turn end. Forced movement does not cancel it. It grants no attack; a legal Bonus Action/extra Action/express attack must supply one. |
| Help | Advantage on a relevant ally check before next regular start, or assist an attack against a foe within 5 ft: the helped ally's first attack before that deadline has advantage. Assistance must be physically possible. |
| Hide / Search | Use the observer-specific procedures below. |
| Ready | Prepare an Action or movement; release uses Reaction as detailed below. |
| Use Object | Pay for the second ordinary interaction or an object's required Action activation. No extra hands/capacity are granted. |
| Improvise | Director declares ability, applicable proficiency, DC/opposed method and intended consequence before rolling. Success does not create unannounced conditions/actions. |

**Brace — NEW.** Action, choose one mode until next regular start. **Intercept:** choose a breach/doorway edge or current melee reach; when a hostile creature charges/leaps/breaches into it and enters legal reach, spend Reaction for one legal melee weapon attack after entry and before later movement/actions. **Hold Ground:** spend Reaction on the first applicable discrete push/pull/shove/knockback to reduce displacement 5 ft, minimum 0. It does not reduce damage, prevent independent Prone, stop gravity/fall steps, currents, conveyors, collapsing ground or teleportation, or grant support/hover. One Brace supplies one opportunity, not an extra Reaction.

**Rally — NEW.** Action; choose one ally within 30 ft able to perceive the rally and one active source-qualified Frightened record. CHA check at that record's source DC, otherwise 10; Success/Critical Success ends only that record. A later reviewed morale rule can supply a separate specific interaction. No automatic temporary HP, loyalty, belief or advantage.

**Withdraw.** Action; separate half-current-Speed movement budget, normal terrain costs, no opportunity attacks from that movement and +2 AC only while that movement resolves. Speed 0 grants none. Moving zero grants no lingering +2. Ordinary movement before/after remains separate. The separate budget is NEW interpretation of Core 480.

**Secure Cargo.** Core 482 preserves the wording “up to one Extraction Load class.” The subsequently reviewed [body and carrying guide](BODY_EQUIPMENT_PLAYTEST.md) supplies the provisional interpretation: spend an Action on one legal attach, lift, transfer or protective operation involving one actual cargo unit or physically constituted bundle with an assigned Extraction Class. Load measures burden; Extraction Class measures danger and logistics. Apply actual capacity, custody, hands, harnesses, checks and durability. The Action grants no extra capacity, equipment, automatic success, invulnerability or reduction in Extraction Class. Full equipment and extraction catalogs remain open.

## Ready

Spend an Action to declare a perceivable trigger and one legal Action-time action or movement. It expires next regular own-turn start. **NEW:** only one pending Ready per creature; replacing it loses the prior setup and any paid held-spell costs. No Bonus Action/Reaction ability, long ritual or multi-minute activity can be prepared by generic Ready.

The trigger happens first, then the readied response immediately follows before later events, unless a specific effect explicitly interrupts. On release spend the normal Reaction and recheck target, range, sight/perception, line of effect, ammunition and other prerequisites. An invalid or declined occurrence spends no release resource; the Ready can wait for another valid occurrence before expiry. This does not undo setup costs. Once released, it is consumed.

For ordinary nonspell actions, feature/ammunition costs are paid on release unless text charges setup. A readied **Attack Action** gets its legally available ordinary attack count; a feature limited to your own turn does not thereby work off-turn. Core Overwatch is explicitly one ranged attack, not that full Attack Action and not Soldier's distinct engine.

A readied spell is actually **cast at preparation**: choose spell/mode, verify legality, pay RP/material/other costs, provide components, resolve Dissonance/Refrain and cast-trigger effects once. Counterspell/cast-interruption opportunity occurs then, not again at release. Every held spell requires concentration, even ordinarily non-concentration spells; incompatible prior concentration ends. Lost concentration loses the held spell and costs. NEW: select the legal target/area point at release and recheck legality without repaying. At release the hold continues as ordinary spell concentration if required, otherwise ends after resolution. Expired/replaced/countered held spells do not refund paid costs.

Ready movement grants a separate current-Speed budget on release, pays terrain costs and can provoke ordinary opportunity attacks. It grants no extra Action, Bonus Action or interaction.

Focused stance's Reaction restriction also blocks an ordinary Ready release; Ready does not create a Counterspell exception. Holding a spell does not waive any current Reaction prohibition.

**Spell-effect duration begins at release.** Its normal duration is anchored when the effect manifests, while Ready expiry and held concentration already run from preparation. A source explicitly tying a particular consequence to casting/preparation retains that consequence's stated clock. No casting cost, Refrain, Dissonance or cast trigger occurs a second time merely from release.

## Stances

Choose one stance during a **regular** own turn before the first event that would use any stance benefit/restriction: attack, spell, relevant check/save or movement. If none occurs, choose before turn end. No retroactive selection. It lasts until next regular start. NEW: extra turns preserve the stance and spent allowances; they cannot choose/replenish it.

| Stance | Effect |
|---|---|
| Aggressive | +2 damage once per qualifying weapon attack's damage packet; −2 AC. Not periodic or copied damage. |
| Guarded | +1 AC, advantage on explicit roll/contest resisting Shove, Speed −5 (minimum 0). Its +1 can coexist with Guard's first-attack +2, giving +3 then +1. This combination is explicitly allowed here. |
| Mobile | First 5 ft of actual voluntary movement does not provoke; no free movement is granted. Your opportunity attacks reduce each base weapon die one step: d12→d10→d8→d6→d4 (minimum d4). Rider dice unchanged. Die sequence is NEW. |
| Focused | Choose +1 save DC for one spell OR +1 to one attack roll from one spell. After use, no-Reaction-except-Counterspell restriction remains through expiry. This grants no Counterspell knowledge, free cost or extra Reaction. |
| Carrying | Choose one actual currently applicable cargo penalty to ignore; choice fixed through expiry. It does not increase capacity or erase all load limits. DEX saves have disadvantage. |

## Maneuvers and ordinary attacks

All opposed checks use Core total comparison: higher wins, exact tie preserves state. No natural-20 automatic victory or hidden task-degree effect on an opposed tie. A printed save-based class maneuver keeps its own method.

| Maneuver | Execution |
|---|---|
| Grapple | Replace one Attack Action attack; reach and target ≤one size larger. STR Athletics vs defender STR Athletics/DEX Acrobatics. Escape Action uses same opposed contest. NEW one free usable grasping hand/appendage per target; release freely on own turn. Ends if required reach breaks or grappler becomes Incapacitated. No concentration required. Dragging halves Speed unless target ≥two sizes smaller. Magical/card exceptions remain source-specific. |
| Shove | Replace one attack; same reach/size/contest limits; success pushes 5 ft away or knocks Prone. |
| Trip | Replace one melee attack, Athletics vs Athletics/Acrobatics, success Prone. NEW same one-size-larger cap. |
| Disarm | Replace one attack, Athletics or weapon check vs Athletics/Acrobatics. Item must be held and reachable. Attacker chooses legal adjacent unoccupied drop space, otherwise target's own space. Explicit non-droppable bonds prevent it; magic/curse alone does not. No severed attunement/ownership/curse. |
| Feint | Bonus Action Deception vs Insight; success advantage on next melee attack this turn only. |
| Sunder | Attack an object/shield/armor component/construct joint using its actual durability system rather than creature HP. Unspecified durability remains an equipment dependency. |
| Break Formation | Special Shove replacing one attack, same reach/size contest with attacker disadvantage. Success pushes 10 ft; snapshot target's adjacent allies before push and remove their Guard Action benefits. Does not remove Guarded stance or target's own Guard. |
| Overwatch | Ready one ranged attack against movement in a named lane. Reaction and ammunition paid at release. Distinct from Soldier's class engine. |

**Pin and Silence the Caster — NEW tradeoff.** Declare before rolling and take **−2 to that attack roll**. Each creature has one Pin token and one Silence the Caster token between starts of its regular turns. An unused token may be spent on any otherwise legal qualifying attack, including off-turn, extra-turn or readied attacks. A legal declaration followed by an attack roll consumes the token even on a miss; an illegal declaration rejected before rolling consumes nothing. Tokens refresh only at regular own-turn start, never on an extra turn or numbered-round boundary. One attack cannot combine another optional tactical maneuver. These provisional numbers require table playtesting.

Pin uses a ranged weapon attack. On hit deal ordinary damage and apply Speed −10 (floor 0), DC `8 + PB + attack ability modifier`. Ordinary Pins do not stack the penalty. At target's regular start, one optional STR Athletics task check against the highest active ordinary Pin DC ends all ordinary Pins on Success/Critical Success. Alternatively one Action removes all automatically. Source-specific card Pins do not silently acquire this generic definition or removal scope.

Silence the Caster uses a melee attack. On hit prevent verbal-component Reactions until target's next regular start. If concentrating, make exactly one CON concentration save at `max(10, floor(post-mitigation damage/2))`, including DC10 when damage is zero. This fulfills, rather than duplicates, the normal damage concentration check. It is not Core Silenced: speech and Action-time verbal casting remain possible.

**Interpose.** While Guarding, after an adjacent ally becomes an attack target but before its roll, spend Reaction and become target if legal for that attack (range/type/line/cover). NEW may move **up to** 5 ft using a separate terrain-paying budget, including zero if already legally positioned. Speed0 permits the zero-movement case only. It redirects one attack without cancelling or duplicating it. Guard's unspent +2 applies if this is the first attack against you. Voluntary movement has ordinary opportunity-attack consequences.

**Press.** On a melee hit during your regular turn, arm one target until next regular start. While unconsumed, another qualifying hit during that turn may replace it. If the target voluntarily retreats from an adjacent space, follow up to 5 ft into the vacated space using a separate terrain-paying budget; no Action/BA/Reaction. It can provoke ordinary opportunity attacks. No following teleport/forced movement. Once used, cannot rearm before next regular turn, including during an extra turn. Speed0 prevents movement.

**Opportunity attack.** Spend Reaction for one melee attack against a seen hostile creature voluntarily leaving reach, immediately **before** exit. Forced movement/teleport do not trigger; Disengage suppresses it for its stated turn. Specific reviewed triggers may override these conditions.

**Two-weapon fighting.** After taking Attack and attacking with a Light melee weapon in one hand, spend Bonus Action for one attack with a different Light melee weapon in the other hand. Do not add a positive ability modifier to that attack's damage; include a negative modifier. This grants neither another Bonus Action nor a class feature.

## Movement and geometry

Track **spent movement cost**, not distance, for ordinary turn movement. Mode cap = `(1 + number of legal Dash actions this turn) × current Speed for that mode`; remaining = max(0, cap − spent cost). Switching modes never refunds cost. Recalculate cap on Speed changes, preserving spent cost. Stand from Prone costs floor(half current Speed) and requires nonzero Speed and enough budget. Dropping Prone costs no movement.

For each foot traveled, normal/difficult/severe terrain costs 1/2/3; use strongest terrain only. Add +1 for crawling and +1 for squeezing. Crawl in difficult =3:1; crawl+squeeze in difficult =4:1. A nonhostile occupied space counts as difficult terrain. Enter a hostile creature's space only with ≥two-size difference or an express feature; never willingly end in an occupied space. Squeeze through a space one size smaller, with own attack/DEX-save disadvantage and attacks-against advantage.

Walk10 through difficult spends20; switch to Fly30 leaves10. Stand with Speed30 spends15; switching to Fly30 leaves15. Two legal Dashes at Speed30 give cap90; prior expenditure still counts. Interpose/Press grant5 budget: they cannot enter a difficult 5-ft tile costing10. Their separate budgets never refill ordinary movement. Ready movement uses Speed at release; Withdraw uses half current Speed. All voluntary movement requires nonzero relevant Speed.

Forced movement does not use these ledgers or provoke ordinary opportunity attacks, cannot place creatures in solid matter, and stops at obstacles. Teleport does not traverse intervening terrain or spend Speed unless printed; it requires a legal destination. Speed0 alone does not block forced movement or teleport. Flight with Prone/Speed0/lost support causes Falling unless Hover or an explicit magical support exception applies; use the existing fixed Fall Marker procedure. Reaching support ends Falling.

NEW grid metric: each orthogonal or diagonal neighboring tile is 5 ft; use the same metric for ordinary ranges/areas. No diagonal cut through a corner when both adjacent orthogonal sides are completely blocked. Exact templates, 3D geometry, mounts, chases, jumping, climbing/swimming procedures and currents need further source review.

## Cover, sight, Hide and Search

Keep **visibility**, **known location**, and **line of effect** separate. Seeing through a barrier does not itself permit attacking through it. Best cover only: half +2 AC/DEX saves; three-quarters +5; total prevents direct targeting through that obstruction. Never add +2 and +5. For areas, evaluate line/cover from the area's origin to each target; total cover there excludes a creature unless the effect explicitly travels around/through the obstruction. The origin point itself must be legally targetable.

Within normal ranged range, no range penalty; beyond normal through long, disadvantage; beyond long, illegal. Ranged attacks also have disadvantage when within5ft of a non-Incapacitated hostile that can see the attacker, even if the attacker cannot see that hostile. All advantage/disadvantage follows the single cancellation rule.

NEW **Hide**: permitted only against observers unable to see you clearly or where a feature permits. Roll DEX Stealth once, record natural die and total. Against each observer's passive DC `10 + WIS Perception modifier including applicable proficiency`, use the Core four-degree task system. Meet DC succeeds before natural1/20 degree adjustment; Success/Critical Success establishes Hidden for that observer. No unsourced passive ±5 adjustment for advantage/disadvantage. Actual Search rolls use their normal modifiers.

Hidden means that observer cannot see you and does not know your location. Clear sight or learning your position ends Hidden for that observer. Moving between hiding places can retain Hidden only if no clear sight/revealing event occurs. A failed Hide cannot simply be rerolled unchanged; a materially new hiding position, concealment, distraction, sight-breaking event or express reroll feature is required. Hidden is not a generic immunity or a free stealth roll for every move.

**Search** Action: WIS Perception for sensory detection; INT Investigation for appropriate traces/deduction; Core Arcana/Engineering where relevant. Directly locating a Hidden creature defaults to Perception against its recorded final Stealth total as a static DC, using task degrees/natural shifts. Success reveals location only to the searcher and ends Hidden for that observer, without automatically granting sight. Communicate normally to inform others. Search can be repeated on later turns by paying the Action.

Attacks against unseen targets have disadvantage; unseen attackers have advantage, subject to the **Core Invisible exception**: once its position is known, Invisible's own attack modifiers end for that observer and cannot immediately return solely because it remains Invisible. Independent darkness, smoke, blindness or hiding can still supply modifiers.

Resolve an attack using pre-attack information; afterward reveal location to observers able to perceive its evidence. If an unseen target's location is unknown, do not directly choose it for a creature-target effect. A weapon/projectile can instead attack a suspected space: wrong space automatically misses; correct space uses unseen rules. A “creature you can see” requirement is never met by position knowledge alone. Area effects can target legal points under their own rules.

## Scope and validation

[Reference checks](COMBAT_PLAYTEST_VALIDATION.json) exercise selected budgets, clocks and edge cases. They do not implement map visibility, inventory, feature legality, complete event dispatch, all maneuvers or a live application. No claim of encounter balance or human playtest completion follows from a passing report.

`start_life_sequence` receives already-resolved/arbitrated HP events; it does not perform condition-family arbitration, cure processing, trigger generation, duration expiry, target legality, defense calculations or general simultaneous-event dispatch. `hide_results` answers only whether Hidden is established, collapsing the two success degrees and two failure degrees; it does not retain the full task-degree record. The Reaction helper models the ordinary resource, not additional Reaction grants printed on specific cards.

Remaining work includes cargo terminology/capacity/durability; detailed movement/environment/chases; area templates and sensory abilities; summons/boss full-turn examples; all-source class/spell semantic interactions and specific resource grants; and the whole-game ledger's other acceptance gates.

## Burning

**Preserved Core text:** Take fire damage at end of turn. An action, immersion, or successful Dexterity save ends it.

**Effect:** At end of the affected creature's regular turn its generic record offers 1d4 fire damage to unified family arbitration, unless its source specifies another magnitude. Only the winning stack group deals damage. Damage immunity does not by itself remove Burning.

**Application:** Applied only by an effect that explicitly inflicts Burning. Record the applying effect's DC; if none exists, use DC 10.

**Duration and ending:** Self or adjacent helper may spend one Action to end all active ordinary-extinguishable attached fires on this creature. Full immersion of all burning portions does the same. Explicit protected-source exceptions survive. At each regular target turn end, after due ticks are consumed, each surviving active ongoing-fire record with future damage remaining makes its own binary DEX save against its own source DC or 10. Success ends only that record. An indefinite generic fire counts as having future damage; a finite record with its final tick consumed expires without another save.

**Stacking:** Independent records enter unified ongoing-fire family arbitration. One Action/full immersion clears all ordinary-extinguishable records; DEX saves operate per surviving eligible record, not one selected-source save for all fires.

**Immunities and specific exceptions:** Fire immunity prevents the damage but does not automatically end Burning unless the source or immunity says so. An effect explicitly immune to Burning blocks application.

**Explicit integration decisions:**

- NEW: 1d4 fire fallback, source DC or 10, damage then free ending save; Action/immersion can end before damage.
- NEW: shared periodic timing and strongest-record precedence; adjacent helper and immersion boundaries.
- SUPERSESSION — NEW PROVISIONAL INTEGRATION: The unified Bleeding/ongoing-fire family registry supersedes earlier provisional clauses that selected generic records separately, used ending DC as a damage tie-breaker, cleared all generic Bleeding through one Medicine result, or used one Burning save to clear all generic Burning. Original Core quotations and raw review history remain unchanged. See CUSTOM_CONDITION_BINDINGS.md and the recorded GPT_GENERIC_CUSTOM_ALIGNMENT review.

## Restrained

**Preserved Core text:** Speed 0; attacks against have advantage, own attacks and Dexterity saves have disadvantage.

**Effect:** Speed becomes 0 and cannot benefit from Speed bonuses. Attack rolls against the creature have advantage; its own attack rolls and Dexterity saving throws have disadvantage.

**Application:** Only an effect explicitly inflicting Restrained applies it.

**Duration and ending:** Use the applying effect's printed duration, save or escape rule. If an effect creates a physical restraint and provides no escape procedure, use the existing shared playtest escape procedure only if that source is eligible for it.

**Stacking:** Multiple Restrained applications do not compound penalties, but all independently maintained sources must end before the creature is no longer Restrained.

**Immunities and specific exceptions:** Restrained immunity blocks application. Teleportation does not automatically end a restraint unless it breaks the source relationship or the applying effect says so.
