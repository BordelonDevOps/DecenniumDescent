## What It Is

Deep Scion is an intelligent floor-dweller with territorial, religious, or political motives. Directors should establish what it protects before combat, because defeating it may create a worse enemy or close a valuable route.

## What Experienced Expeditions Notice

Masonry repaired from the inside; water channels marked with votive cuts; discarded fungi sorted by toxicity; warning bolts deliberately embedded at eye level.

### Traits

**Prepared Ground.** It gains advantage on the first attack or check made from a fortified position in its lair.

**Escalation Pulse.** **Pressure Wave** recharges on 5-6; visible signs appear one round before the first use.

### Actions

**Weapon Strike.** *Attack:* +6 to hit, one target. *Hit:* 2d6+3 slashing damage (average 10).

**Pressure Wave (Recharge 5-6).** Creatures in the affected area make a DC 14 Constitution save, taking 4d6 bludgeoning damage (average 14) on a failure and half on a success. The Director should describe the charge-up before resolving it.

## How It Fights

It opens with **Weapon Strike** against the target most capable of controlling its movement. When **Pressure Wave** recharges, it delays only if it can catch a more important cluster or interrupt a decisive ritual. It uses prepared cover and retreats behind the next defensive layer when the first is lost.

## How to Defeat It

**Prepare Before Contact**

- Prepare resistance or mitigation for slashing, bludgeoning damage.
- Spread before **Pressure Wave** becomes available.
- Map doors, bridges, and firing lanes before parley fails.

**Combat Priority**

Deny prepared terrain and command figures; accept surrender when the objective is secured.

**Exploit**

The defining weakness is **None identified**. Treat this as an encounter solution rather than an automatic damage vulnerability. Successful Research, Recon, or Resource actions should make the exploit easier or safer.

**Withdraw When**

Withdraw or change the objective when Pressure Wave catches three or more Expedition members. A failed retreat against this creature usually costs more than abandoning the immediate treasure.

## Habitat, Goals, and Lair

Defended holds use layered doors, murder shafts, water traps, narrow bridges, and negotiated kill zones. Religious sites add chanting pools, toxin vents, and captive witnesses.

The deep folk are not one faction. They are cultures forced into the same trade roads, flooded caverns, and defensive wars. Their encounters should begin with motive before initiative.

Deep folk respond to ownership, passage rights, debts, clan names, and proof of common enemies.
