# Create and advance a character

**Mechanical foundation, provisional campaign-playtest candidate.** The [joint source conference](../../../../docs/audit/2026-10-08/full-game/GPT_CHARACTER_FOUNDATION_CONFERENCE.md) settles the class foundations and advancement rules below. Use the [17-class tables](CHARACTER_FOUNDATION_TABLES.md) beside this guide. Full ancestry traits, background packages and equipment records still need their own reconciliation; this foundation does not certify a complete ready-to-play character or live companion parity.

## Begin a character sheet

1. **Record the concept and first Base class.** Choose one of the 17 classes. Start at character level 1 and that class’s level 1. Keep a chronological level record and the order in which you enter classes. Arch and Hero are later stages within a class, not additional Base classes. The former Fighter/Swashbuckler entries and separate historical prestige records supply no extra starting class or automatic grants.
2. **Generate six abilities.** Choose the method before generating scores: roll four d6, discard the lowest and total the other three, six times; or assign the standard array 15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8. Assign those six results once to STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS and CHA. Keep the original rolls. Do not reroll, mix methods or select a preferred set without a separate table rule. The optional provisional point-buy method below is also available.
3. **Record ancestry and any required variant.** Apply its actual ability increases once after assigning scores, resolving an OR choice once. Record size, movement, senses, languages and each complete trait procedure from its source. Trait names alone are insufficient. Do not stack alternative builder/racial-variant entries onto the same book trait. Full mechanical reconciliation of the 14 ancestries is still pending. This step grants no authority to revise their cosmology or origin lore.
4. **Choose one background.** Record its actual skills, tools, languages, feature and starting package, including required choices. Complete background procedures remain a separate source pass. The source permits a custom background with two skills, two tools/languages, equipment and a Reputation-related feature developed with the Director; that permission does not supply an invented unlimited feature or a second background.
5. **Apply starting training.** Use this first class’s saves, armor, weapons, tools and number of skill choices from the foundation tables. Use the class list for each skill choice. Add ancestry/background training with the duplicate procedure below. Proficiency grants training, not equipment ownership, spells or a second PB on one roll.
6. **Set HP, Hit Dice and proficiency.** First-character-level base HP is the maximum face of this class’s HD; add current CON modifier, minimum 1. Record one Hit Die of that size and its spent state. At level 1 PB is +2. Keep base maximum HP separate from later effects, reductions and systemic multipliers.
7. **Acquire the actual class kit.** Follow the class’s own level-1 engine, automatic grants and scheduled choices. Ordinary classes start with four Base choices, at least two active, where their schedule says so. Druid, Oracle and Paladin have explicit schedules; follow those instead. Make required engine/path choices, such as Oracle Mystery and Curse, exactly when their cards require them. Use the [class library](CLASS_PLAYTEST_INDEX.md), [legal acquisition examples](CLASS_PLAYTEST_BUILD_EXAMPLES.md), and [Paladin oath pairs](PALADIN_OATHS_AND_OATHBREAKERS.md). No legacy Skill Points, companion feature strings or historical prestige rows add grants to this schedule.
8. **Record magic and class resources.** Use the class’s current Core V2 RP table and rank gates, plus its actual known/prepared/spellbook procedure. Record each selected operational spell’s identity and casting class. Enough RP never unlocks a rank or school. Oracle uses Revelation Charges; Soldier and Warrior have no general RP casting. Initial class resources and empty stores follow their actual engine setup; preparation or copying is not waived. Read [shared class rules](CLASS_PLAYTEST_RULES.md) and [spell integration](SPELL_INTEGRATION_RULES.md).
9. **Choose starting equipment or money.** Take the class and background packages, **or** roll the first class’s starting wealth and buy equipment with it. Wealth replaces all starting package equipment, including package money; background training and features remain. Record purchases, remaining coins, ammunition, components, containers, quantities and actual weight/Load. An item must exist with usable statistics; a proficiency or feature label cannot create it. The equipment pass must still settle missing profiles, prices and eligibility tags.
10. **Finish the derived sheet.** Calculate ability modifiers, trained saves and skills, legal equipped AC, movement, weapon attacks, class attacks/DCs and actual resource caps. Choose one applicable AC formula rather than adding formulas. A normal proficient attack is d20 + PB + its actual attack modifier; class DC is 8 + PB + its actual class modifier. Use actual negative modifiers; the class card’s minimum-1 effect modifier is not a minimum attack/DC modifier. Note conditions, Wounds, supplies and other scene state separately. Record the source/card for every nonordinary number.

An ancestry/background feature, spell or item that remains mechanically unresolved is a visible unfinished dependency. A checked box or a saved companion character does not resolve that dependency.

## Abilities and training

Ability modifier = floor((score − 10) / 2), including negative results. For example, 8 gives −1, 13 gives +1 and 16 gives +3.

**Optional new candidate method:** 27-point buy, scores 8–15 before ancestry. Costs are 8:0, 9:1, 10:2, 11:3, 12:4, 13:5, 14:7, 15:9. Spend no more than 27 points across six scores; no score outside that range. This adopts the existing companion method as a provisional option, not a claim that the book already specified it.

Resolve duplicate training as follows:

- For a selectable grant, choose another eligible option from that grant’s own list.
- For a fixed ancestry/background skill grant with no alternative list, choose another of the 20 basic skills.
- For a duplicated tool, choose another eligible tool from that grant’s list; if it has no list, use another actually supported tool in the same broad category.
- Duplicate armor, weapon, save or multiclass chassis training gives no skill or feat compensation. A new multiclass skill selection must still use its own permitted list.
- Duplicate proficiency never creates Expertise unless an exact feature says so. If no legal unowned choice exists, record the unresolved selection; it is not permission to substitute a feat, invent a proficiency, or bank a class-card selection.

PB is applied once to a check even if a skill and tool both qualify. An exact Expertise multiplier modifies that one proficiency contribution; it does not add another independent PB.

## Gain a level

Opening campaigns officially use milestones. The Director’s announced milestones grant advancement; a CR-to-XP UI helper is not the official Opening reward law. The book’s cumulative XP thresholds remain available for tables using optional XP. See [XP provenance](XP_ADVANCEMENT_CANON.md).

Each earned advancement adds one total character level, to a combined maximum of 20. Choose one existing Base class to advance, or enter one new Base class if eligible. Process multiple earned levels one at a time; do not skip intervening choices.

1. Record the gained class and new class level. Recalculate total level and PB: +2 at total 1–4, +3 at 5–8, +4 at 9–12, +5 at 13–16, +6 at 17–20.
2. Add one Hit Die of the gained class’s size. For HP, roll that die or use its fixed rounded-up average: d6→4, d8→5, d10→6, d12→7. Record the base result before CON. This includes taking a new class’s first level after character level 1; maximum first-level HP is never granted again.
3. Apply exactly the gained class’s acquisition row, automatic improvements and class-level prerequisites. Complete its choices and any newly reached Arch/Hero gate. Automatic grants, scheduled selections and ASIs are distinct; do not pay a selection twice or grant an extra selection because the same feature appears in a legacy table.
4. At an ASI milestone, increase one score by 2 or two scores by 1, normally no higher than 20, or select one eligible operational feat. Ordinary milestones are class levels 4/8/12/16/19; Oracle uses 4/8/14/19. Follow any already-reviewed specific exception. No starting feat or repeatable feat is implied. [Eight complete feat options](FEAT_PLAYTEST_INDEX.md) do not make the other 327 complete.
5. Update legal repertoire, prepared capacity, class scaling and resource maxima at the gained **class** level. Use class-specific Druid preparation, Wizard spellbook/matrices and other explicit rules before generic acquisition defaults. New capacity does not provide an unnamed spell, an unearned choice, a filled Echo record or an extra prepared spell without the required preparation procedure.
6. Recompute base maximum HP from all attained-level records using current CON: sum(max(1, each stored base value + current CON modifier)). Then apply the separate [maximum-HP ledger](MAXIMUM_HP_PLAYTEST.md), including compatible bonuses, reductions and systemic factors. Do not feed an already modified maximum back in as the base.
7. Keep current HP and spent resources unchanged except for a downward clamp when a maximum falls. An ordinary level-up is not healing or a rest: it cannot revive a downed/dead character, erase Wounds or refill already spent Hit Dice, RP, RC, class pools, item charges or uses. Newly granted features still follow an explicit acquisition/initialization clause if they have one; “gain a maximum” alone does not mean “restore current.”

**New HP completion:** each level’s HP contribution has a minimum of 1. Recompute those individual floors after permanent CON changes. This preserves retroactive CON adjustment without a naive level×modifier-change formula that can bypass a floor. Capacity increases grant no current HP; decreases can clamp current HP without damage/healing triggers, consistent with the reviewed maximum-HP interface. Temporary ability effects follow their own actual source rules rather than being silently treated as permanent advancement.

No banked card selections, free retraining or free respec is introduced. Use an existing explicit replacement/retraining procedure where one exists.

## Multiclass entry and class features

When entering a new Base class, meet the multiclass score prerequisites for **every held class and the new class**. The foundation tables give the exact source gates; a class’s descriptive primary ability is not a substitute. These requirements are checked on new-class entry. Losing a qualifying score later does not erase earned classes/features, though it can prevent entering another new class. Advancing a class already held is not a new-class entry test.

Only the first character class grants its full starting saves and proficiency package. Each later newly entered class grants its separate multiclass proficiency row once. It grants no second background, starting wealth/equipment or full starting skill count. Its genuine level-1 engine/features and acquisition schedule still apply.

**New chassis clarification:** a level-1 card whose sole function is to restate starting armor/weapon proficiency cannot bypass the multiclass table. Paladin Martial Proficiency therefore supplies no heavy-armor bypass. An independent non-chassis mechanic in a feature remains available on its actual terms.

Use the largest equivalent Extra Attack count, never their sum. Use one applicable AC formula, never their sum. Duplicate historical Channel Divinity labels do not manufacture another use pool. Distinct operational class engines and paid techniques remain distinct and obey their own costs and the shared action limits.

Class level controls rank access, acquisitions, S scaling, Arch/Hero gates and finales. Total character level controls PB and optional XP thresholds. A Bard 5 / Mage 5 is total 10 with PB +4, but neither class has reached its Hero gate.

## One shared RP pool

Calculate each eligible class’s own current candidate RP pool at its own class level. Sort these classes by:

1. Caster tier: Full (Cleric, Druid, Mage, Wizard), then Half (Paladin, Ranger), then Hybrid (Bard, Shaman, Mirrorsage, Crimson Agent), then Minor (Dragoon, Monk, Thief, Tinker).
2. Within a tier, higher class level first.
3. If class levels tie, higher actual RP pool first.
4. If both tie, the class acquired earlier first. Record that order; do not choose a different primary per cast.

Oracle, Soldier and Warrior contribute no RP pool. Maximum RP = the first class’s full pool + ceil(one quarter of the second class’s pool). With only one eligible class use its pool; with none, maximum RP is 0. Third and later eligible classes contribute no extra capacity, while retaining their earned class features and legal repertoire. Use current Core V2 candidate pools, not the older smaller companion-derived tables.

All RP-paid spells and techniques use this one merged pool unless an exact rule names another resource. RC, BG, Momentum, Ki, Conviction, Lay on Hands and other class resources remain separate. A change in the primary class or maximum is not recovery: preserve current RP and clamp only if above the new maximum. This can reduce maximum RP when a newly entered higher-tier class becomes primary; do not silently choose a larger lower-tier pool instead.

Each ordinary cast declares its casting class before costs. That class must independently know/prepare the spell, meet its school/rank gate and supply its own casting ability. Another class’s levels or repertoire cannot fill those requirements. Where two classes know the same spell, choosing its casting class does not create a second spell identity.

## Shared strain, recovery and concentration

Keep one character-wide Dissonance number and one Refrain history across classes. For a cast, evaluate limit = max(4, 2 + total-character PB + max(0, the actual casting-class modifier)). Apply the existing surcharges, ranked-cast cutoff, repetition and explicit class exceptions from [shared class rules](CLASS_PLAYTEST_RULES.md). Switching casting class does not clear either history; the same spell identity still repeats. Class cadence benefits require their actual ownership and trigger conditions. Idle decay and Grounding occur once per character turn, not once per class.

Generic Retune happens once per eligible completed short rest for the character. It restores max(0, round-half-up(maximum RP / 4) + the current primary RP class’s casting modifier), capped at the merged maximum, and clears Dissonance. Optional once/day meditation uses that same primary class’s positive modifier and the existing PB-based strain reduction. Long-rest RP recovery restores the merged pool under the existing recovery rules. None of these generic procedures duplicates because the character has more classes or recovers a different named resource without its own rule.

Oracle Revelations use RC and do not generate ordinary Weave strain unless expressly stated; they do not clear existing strain/Refrain from another class. One ordinary concentration slot is shared across classes, with only the exact already-reviewed linked-effect exceptions such as Spell Tether, Tethered Cast and Crimson Harmony. No generic Overcast option is reintroduced.

## Worked foundation checks

- **First Bard level:** CON 14 gives modifier +2; base HP 8+2 = 10, one d8 Hit Die and PB +2. Choose three class skills from the 20-skill list and four eligible Base cards with at least two active, as the Bard schedule directs. Background/ancestry grants remain separate and must be fully specified before play.
- **Bard 1→2 without a rest:** fixed HP base 5 and CON +2 increase base maximum 10→17. If current HP was 6 it stays 6. The second Bard level grants its actual two Base selections. A higher RP maximum does not refill spent RP.
- **New class does not repeat maximum HP:** a Bard 3 taking Mage 1 adds d6 roll or fixed 4, plus CON; it does not add 6 merely because Mage is class level 1. Total PB is still +2 at character level 4. The character must meet Bard CHA 13 and Mage INT 13 before entry.
- **Retroactive CON with floors:** a d6 first level recorded as 6 and a later d6 roll recorded as 1 give contributions 4 and 1 at CON 6 (−2), total 5. At CON 8 (−1) they give 5 and 1, total 6. Adding a naive two levels × +1 would incorrectly give 7.
- **A three-class caster:** Cleric 6, Druid 6 and Wizard 1 use Cleric/Druid as the two Full-tier contributors because class level precedes pool size. Their own level-6 pools are 15 each, giving 15+ceil(15/4) = 19 RP; earlier acquisition breaks their tie. Wizard 1 adds no RP but retains its own legal Wizard 1 spell access. Total 13 supplies PB +5; no class has a Hero gate.
- **Different casting stats:** at total level 6, PB +3, a modifier +3 caster has limit 8 and a modifier +1 caster has limit 6. Shared Dissonance 6 is below the first limit but at the second. Casting through the second class uses its surcharge; the shared strain number and spell repetition history survive the switch.
- **Monk wealth:** rolling 5d4 for a total of 12 gives 12 gp, not 120 gp. No background-package money is added if this money method replaces the packages.
- **Later Paladin:** a non-Paladin’s entry uses the Paladin multiclass row. Its level-1 Martial Proficiency restatement grants no extra heavy armor or second set of saving-throw proficiencies.
