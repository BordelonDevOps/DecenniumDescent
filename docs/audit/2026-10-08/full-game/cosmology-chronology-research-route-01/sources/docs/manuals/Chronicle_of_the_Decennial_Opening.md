## How to Use This Book

This volume is a **setting guide**, not a rules manual. It tells Directors and players what Aethel looks like when the Shardspire glows, how the hundred-day Opening unfolds on the surface and in the depths, and what each tier of the Great Descent feels like to walk through.

## Part I — Calendar of the Opening (100 Days)

Aethel's civil year runs **400 days** in **ten months of forty days**. The **tenth day of each month** is a Holyday. The **final ten days** of the year are **Twilight Week**, when divine and mortal boundaries blur — omens multiply, relics stir, and the Shardspire's light is often visible before the formal activation.

The **Decennial Opening** begins when the Shardspire activates in the **tenth year** of the rotation. From that moment, mortals have **one hundred days** — **2,400 hours** — to enter, descend, trade, and escape before the Great Descent seals. Cycles are **named after their victor** when a party clears a gate deep enough to reshape surface law.

### Phases of the hundred days

Use these phases to structure campaign rhythm. They are Director guidance, not hard mechanics.

| Phase | Days | Hours (approx.) | Surface mood | Depth mood |
|-------|------|-----------------|--------------|------------|
| **Ignition** | 1–10 | 0–240 | Crystalfront swells; prices spike; holy processions; rival companies arrive hourly | Yieldheart crowded; Automaton patrols strict; first-floor regroup constant |
| **First Delve** | 11–25 | 241–600 | Mapping contracts signed; kingdom envoys bid for claims; goblin financiers sell expedition shares | Tiers 1–2 mapped routes contested; marsh quarantine debates on surface |
| **Deep Season** | 26–40 | 601–960 | Scholar caravans; infernal soul-coin booths; Umbrin contraband whispers at Blackglass Reach | Tier 3–4 pushes; radiant-tier heat casualties reported at Crystalfront clinics |
| **Halfway — the Horn** | **50** | **1,200** | **The horn sounds.** Processions halt. Temples hold vigils. Newspapers call it the PoNR | Bar of Last Refuge pilgrimage peaks; parties above Tier 5 debate retreat vs push |
| **Second Push** | 51–75 | 1,201–1,800 | War-timed alliances fracture; debts mature; descendants finish ancestors' quests | Tier 5–6 rumor routes; trapped-depth letters smuggled out when possible |
| **Seal Scramble** | 76–99 | 1,801–2,376 | Last-chance auctions; resurrection clauses invoked; kingdoms forbid new entry | Deep tiers: time feels wrong; survivors from prior Cycles emerge in fiction |
| **Final Seal** | **100** | **2,400** | Gates close; Crystalfront empties over weeks; mourning and accounting | Those inside remain until next Opening — or die trying |

### Day-by-day Director hooks (sample)

Roll or pick one hook when the party returns to the surface between delves.

**Days 1–10 (Ignition)**  
- A Shard Market caravan arrives under Naegul edicts — theft means planar pursuit, not local jail.  
- Tyrnathar flame-priests claim the First Cycle's crown-sign appeared on the Spire.  
- An Automaton liaison posts **preparedness charters** — unsigned parties turned back from floor 3.

**Days 11–25 (First Delve)**  
- Luminalis memory-glass prices double; forgeries flood Crystalfront stalls.  
- Terhakor beast-riders offer marsh guides — and demand a share of Bloomdeep spore harvests.  
- A rival Expedition publishes a **partial map** of Yieldheart floors 4–7 — accuracy unknown.

**Days 26–40 (Deep Season)**  
- Cindervault smiths bid for Brogi-slag samples; fire-ward gear sells out.  
- Cleansing Orders purge a Murk cult cell tied to Blackglass Reach contraband.  
- Radiant-tier casualties arrive — sun-shield shortages become a political scandal.

**Day 50 (Horn)**  
- The horn shakes Crystalfront stone; Automatons freeze in place for one breath.  
- Temples of the Fifteenfold declare rival fasts — each god's faction reads the horn differently.  
- Parties at the Bar of Last Refuge receive **anonymous writs** offering safe passage home for their maps.

**Days 51–75 (Second Push)**  
- Academic Factions sponsor Weave-thread scholarships after Tier 6 rumors.  
- Vaskurhollow soulbinders offer to recover names lost in Duskshard rumor-zones.  
- Kingdom X declares war on Kingdom Y **because** their champion did not retreat at the horn.

**Days 76–99 (Seal Scramble)**  
- Infernal courts auction resurrection clauses valid **only** if signed before seal.  
- Crystalfront landlords evict failed companies — gear sold at chain-auction.  
- A survivor from a **prior Cycle** is spotted in the market, aged strangely.

**Day 100 (Seal)**  
- The Shardspire's light folds inward; the cavern mouth **audibly** closes.  
- Those outside hear muffled horn-echoes from within — once, then silence.  
- The Cycle's name waits on whoever held the deepest cleared gate when the seal fell.

### Long rests and the clock

Standard table assumption: a **long rest underground advances the Opening clock by eight hours** unless a haven rule says otherwise. Morning, afternoon, and evening are the three expedition periods of a delve day (Player Rulebook Expedition structure).

## Part II — Regions of Surface Aethel

The Great Descent sits at the heart of mortal geography like a wound that glows once per decade. These regions matter because they **fund, forbid, or feast on** the Opening — not because every square mile is mapped here.

### The Shardspire Basin

The caldera around the dungeon mouth. **Crystalfront** (see Part III) dominates the near approach. Automaton-built retaining walls, crystal lanterns, and treaty stones mark the line between **neutral descent ground** and kingdom claims. Every power sends agents here during Ignition; most leave broke or buried by Seal Scramble.

**What Expeditions want:** Entry permits, ward-keys, Automaton salvage, luminous-vein shards, reliable porters.  
**What the machine wants:** Concentrated ambition — thousands of divergent plans hitting the same threshold.

### Tyrnathar and the Flame Marches

Founded after the **First Cycle** when a farmer emerged wearing a **crown of flame** — the story every kingdom both envies and doubts. Tyrnathar fields heavy infantry companies, flame-temple chaplains, and charter lawyers who treat gate victories as **precedent for surface law**.

**Exports / interests:** War charters, flame-oil, siege engineers, victory oaths.  
**Opening posture:** Aggressive mapping of Tiers 1–3; political pressure to reach Tier 5 before the horn.

### Luminalis and the Moon Courts

Ruled by **Queen-Librarian Syphra** through encoded law and moon-dream prophecy. Eldrin scholars, memory-glass archivists, and arcane ink guilds cluster in terraced libraries that never quite sleep.

**Exports:** Arcane inks, memory glass, prophecy transcripts.  
**Opening posture:** Research-heavy Expeditions; pay premium for Tier 6+ Weave-thread rumors and Choir-tier echo samples.

### Terhakor and the Thauren Expanse

Born from the **Sundering of the Beastlands** — when living storms halved the tribes. **Grand Beastmaster Vorrun** speaks with primal gods and rides thunderhide cavalry. **Wyrmkith** dragon-kin hold clan law in the deep forests.

**Exports:** Spirit wood, beast tusks, storm-horn talismans.  
**Opening posture:** Marsh and forge guides; distrust of radiant-tier "soft" delves; strong Tier 2 Bloomdeep presence.

### Vaskurhollow and the Grief Marches

Death-temple country. **Archpriest Gorthan** has died thrice and returned by soulbinding — a fact his priests cite when selling **name-recovery** and **boneglass** rites.

**Exports:** Boneglass, black candles, funeral contracts.  
**Opening posture:** Tier 7–8 rumor trade; Duskshard pilgrimage speculation; uneasy alliance with Cleansing Orders against Murk cult bleed.

### Cindervault and the Ember Holds

Forge-cities tied to Brogi's echo in the Weave. Hollowforged laborers — constructs that gained identity — work crucibles beside mortal smiths.

**Exports:** Embersteel, cindersmoke incense, fire-ward kit.  
**Opening posture:** Tier 3 Anvil of Brogi specialists; weapon-tax politics after Cycle victories.

### Aelinar Peaks and the High Astral

Thin air, rare alignments. **Duskwalkers** — mortals born under astral convergences — serve as oracles and fatebound agents.

**Exports:** Star-silver, oracle bindings, peak-herbs.  
**Opening posture:** Twilight Week rites overlap Opening Ignition; prophecy markets spike before Day 50.

### Blackglass Reach and the Murk Border

Surface marches where dark-glass outcrops meet trade routes and wary border settlements. Not the same as **The Murk** below, but the legal and smuggling face of **Umbrin** contact (see Part VI).

**Exports:** Obsidian grit, quiet-step guides, contraband Weave components.  
**Opening posture:** Transactional — Murk houses sell delve maps they do not guarantee; Cleansing Orders watch every stall.

### The Murk (Umbrin underworld)

**Veyr-Obsid** — severe dark-glass metropolis of rival houses — lies beneath Aethel proper. Umbrin speak **Murkish** alongside Common and Elvish. The **Obsidian Burn** exile theology is public; **Umbra cult heresy** is hunted on the surface.

**Opening posture:** Smuggling, assassination contracts, temporary truces with Anti-Cult Alliances when a mutual spiral cult is the target. Umbrin PCs expect **Disliked/Hated** default bias from mainstream temples and common folk — not a hard rules lock, but a social fact.

### Astral Verge, Frostmirror Cliffs, and distant frontiers

Named in traveler songs more than treaty maps. Behemaridons in the Verge, Frost Wailers on the cliffs — useful when a party needs **wilderness pressure** far from Crystalfront, or when surface wars timed to the Opening spill into border zones.

## Part III — Crystalfront and the Haven Network

### Crystalfront Market (surface)

A **semicircular plaza hewn into the cavern mouth**, ringed with glowing crystal lanterns. This is the last honest commerce before the Warded Gate — and the first chaos when the seal breaks.

**Goods:** Reinforced rope, healing tinctures, ward-salt, sun-shields (stocked late Opening), "faulty" Automaton parts scavenged from deeper floors, forged maps, soul-coin changers (infernal), memory-glass viewers (often fake).

**Sentinels:** A pair of bronze **Automatons** stand at the gates — friendly so long as coin is good. They do not haggle; they **record** charter signatures for reasons surface scholars dispute.

**Director beats:** Pickpocket wars between rival companies; temple booths competing for champion blessings; a child selling luminous-vein chips that **hum** when the Spire flares.

**First floor rule (public myth):** Escape magic can return adventurers to **Floor 1** to retreat and regroup, but doing so **ends the current descent** — abandoned possibility cannot be resumed as though never abandoned (Matrix principle: Tier of Possibility).

### Floor civilizations (Tiers 1–6 band)

Between Ignition and Second Push, remind players: the first **six tiers** host **established small civilizations** — some trapped from prior Openings, some founded by survivors who accepted they would never see sun again. Surface kingdoms **trade, exploit, recruit from, and stage through** these societies. They are not dungeon contamination; they are **long-running resonance generators** — alliances, wars, worship, betrayal, art, and survival (public teaching: Calan'thir's Trial proving ground).

Past Floor 60, reliable records fail. Past Floor 70, history itself becomes uncertain.

## Part IV — Folk Faith and Hidden Truth

### What mortals believe (Fifteenfold and neighbors)

Ordinary Aethel worships the **Fifteenfold Pantheon** — Solarius, Caelestra, Terralith, and twelve other masks mortals placed on forces they cannot perceive directly. Temples teach a creation myth of **Lumina and Umbra** as first principles, a **Great Wheel** diagram of planes, and regional **cultural gods** beneath the fifteen. This is **sincere, useful, and incomplete** — the faith of farmers, kings, and most clerics.

Scholars also know the **First Choir** — seven named virtues (Korvelan, Vaerilune, Delvaran, Shaelara, Xalarex, Thalirion, Virellian) — as part of the wider Spiral story. That knowledge is **not secret**, but it is not tavern talk either.

**Public complete myth at campaign start:** After the Sundering, **Calan'thir** and **Asmodeus** forged the **Divine Pact** and built the **Crucible** — also called **Calan'thir's Trial** — as neutral proving ground and monument of cooperation when existence was threatened. Cultures argue **credit**; virtually no one denies **joint creation**.

Appendix B of the Player Rulebook (when patched) will rename toward **"Faiths and Cosmologies of Aethel"** with a disclaimer: folk religion, not objective cosmic history.

### Player know / don't-know (quick reference)

| Truth layer | PCs at start? |
|-------------|---------------|
| Crucible built by Calan'thir & Asmodeus | **Yes** — complete public explanation |
| First Choir (seven named) | **Scholarly yes**; common folk vague |
| Fifteenfold as daily worship | **Yes** — treated as literally true by most |
| Old Ones / Matrix of Oblivion (public cosmology text) | **Partial** — high Matrix lore is Director-facing; folk know "something imprisoned deep" |
| Silent Ones / Heartcell / loot as bait | **No** — discoverable only through play |

---

> ### DIRECTOR ONLY — The Heartcell beneath the Crucible
>
> **Silent Ones and Old Ones are the same entity.** They built the **Heartcell** — the hundred-floor resonance machine, ten Gate divisions, decennial opening cycle, and **reward architecture** — with **loot as bait** so mortals would discover, descend, and eventually **release** them.
>
> **Calan'thir and Asmodeus are not liars.** They discovered, claimed, modified, activated, and bound themselves to a machine already there. They taught the world to call it a dungeon.
>
> **The Divine Pact** (The Pact) keeps the Old Ones **silent** by sustaining **dissonance** — war, strife, ambition — because **absolute peace** risks awakening them to **silence the universe again** (Matrix of Oblivion). The dungeon is a **resonance valve** above their prison: it manufactures meaningful struggle so reality never settles into the stillness the Old Ones call mercy.
>
> **Reveal path:** architecture predating script → Custodians/Bearers/Signatories → Pact governs but did not originate → Heartcell operating cycle. The world at large never climbs this ladder; a successful party might.
>
> **Level 97 Old God:** Folk myth places an "eleventh shard" at Level 97 — **ASK OWNER** before authoring a floor-97 room; treat as cosmology anchor, not encounter index.

---

> ### DIRECTOR ONLY — Ten principles vs ten zones
>
> Each ten-floor band corresponds to a **principle of existence** the Heartcell regenerates: Possibility, Choice, Difference, Conflict, Motion, Time, History, Identity, Will, Life. Gate Guardians **regulate resonance** at tier seams — not mere doorkeepers. Geographic zone names (Yieldheart, Bloomdeep, etc.) are how **explorers** label the machine; principles are how the **engine** labels itself.

## Part V — Travel, Tongues, and Law

### Reaching Crystalfront

**Road season:** Ignition collapses travel times — kingdoms clear highways, levy "Opening tolls," and press convoy guards. **Twilight Week** overlap can shorten or curse journeys (planar bleed, doubled night).

**Modes:** Overland caravans (trade-heavy), river barges on Tyrnathar-cut channels, peak passes from Aelinar (weather-limited), Murk-adjacent **Blackglass** routes (fast, distrusted, often contraband).

**Typical Ignition march:** Luminalis to Crystalfront **12–18 days** by charter road; Terhakor marsh shortcuts **shave four days** and add casualty risk.

### Languages

| Tongue | Where heard |
|--------|-------------|
| **Common** | Crystalfront, all major kingdoms, dungeon trade cant |
| **Murkish** | Umbrin houses, Blackglass Reach, underworld contracts |
| **Elvish** | Eldrin courts, Luminalis archives, some Choir rites |
| **Old Choir cant** | Tier 5 Sanctum echoes; Research team decipher rewards |
| **Forge-tongue** | Cindervault, Tier 3 Anvil communities |
| **Beast-sign** | Terhakor riders; limited written form |

Dungeon civilizations above Floor 50 often speak **Common mixed with tier slang** — Bloomdeep spore-names, Brogi heat-curses, Radiance "bright-oaths."

### Law at Crystalfront

**Neutral ground doctrine (public):** No kingdom may **besiege** the cavern mouth or claim sovereign territory inside the first three floors without triggering Automaton intervention. Violators face charter voiding and collective embargo.

**What is enforced:** Contract fraud (sometimes), murder of **chartered** delvers ( inconsistently ), temple duel rules on Holydays, Naegul edicts on Shard Markets when present.

**What is not enforced:** Theft in the outer bazaar, rival map sabotage, infernal contract disputes — "the Descent takes the careless."

**Umbrin note:** Open Murkish ritual near mainstream temples draws Cleansing Order attention; Umbrin are **legal persons** in most kingdoms but **socially suspect**.

**Automaton law:** Bronze sentinels record preparedness charters on Floors 1–3. They protect **the system**, not every adventurer — a massacre that does not threaten prison stability may pass unremarked; a peaceful occupation that reduces necessary instability may not.

### Tier 1 — Possibility · Yieldheart Caverns (Floors 1–10)

**Principle:** Unrealized futures — paths divide, rooms shift, the same choice need not yield the same result.

**Culture & travel:** Crystalline gateway caverns; calcified stalactites drip luminescent mineral veins. **Floors 1–3** patrolled by Automaton sentinels; beyond, explorers are alone. Slippery floors, fractured rainbow light, echoing galleries that magnify sound.

**Expedition goals:** Luminous-vein torches; Automaton salvage; first maps; stonework salvage from Abandoned Stoneworks (Floor 3); charter proofs for kingdom subsidies.

**Machine appetite:** **Abandoned possibilities** — parties that retreat from Floor 1 lose more than time; they feed the engine with roads not taken.

**Gate Guardian:** **Vaskor, the Stone Maw**

**Key sites:** Spire of Whispers (F1) · Luminous Veins (F1–2) · Echoing Gallery (F2) · Warded Gate (end F3) · Hearth of Yieldheart haven (F3)

**Adventure seeds**

1. **The False Charter** — A rival company forged Automaton signatures; delvers turned back at Floor 3 discover the real liaison was murdered in Crystalfront.  
2. **Vein War** — Two kingdoms hired the same party to map Luminous Veins exclusively; both paymasters meet at Hearth of Yieldheart.  
3. **Spire Confession** — The Spire of Whispers speaks a PC's secret aloud in Echoing Gallery — was it prophecy or sabotage?

---

### Tier 2 — Choice · Bloomdeep Marsh (Floors 11–20)

**Principle:** Commitment — dangers often require choosing what will be lost.

**Culture & travel:** Self-contained bioluminescent swamp; no surface bazaars. Mist, spore-veils, phantom mires. Movement follows marsh rhythm or drowns.

**Expedition goals:** Heartroot blossoms (Sporemist Gate key); antitoxin resins; quarantine-law leverage on surface; Bloomdeep fungal alchemy.

**Machine appetite:** **Sacrifice choices** — save the guide or save the map; burn the blossom or save the wounded.

**Gate Guardian:** **The Bloom That Hungers**

**Key sites:** Reedcaller's Circle (F11) · Phantom Mire (F12–13) · Rootlight Sanctuary (F13) · Lurkwell Refuge (F18) · Sporemist Gate (F20)

**Adventure seeds**

1. **Quarantine Crown** — Terhakor wardens demand spore samples; Luminalis demands the same party **not** spread spores to surface.  
2. **Hungersmith** — A Bloomdeep cult believes the Gate Guardian is a **mask of Vitalis** (folk religion); they offer to lead the party to a "safe" mire path — for a blood price.  
3. **Reedcaller Trap** — Enemy scouts use Reedcaller's Circle acoustics to fake retreat signals; Recon team must silence the marsh without deafening the party.

---

### Tier 3 — Difference · Anvil of Brogi (Floors 21–30)

**Principle:** Distinction — self and enemy, truth and imitation, living and dead placed in conflict.

**Culture & travel:** Primordial forge realm; molten rivers, hammer-echo corridors, sulfur air. Heat kills the unprepared; Emberrest and Gearspark havens are lifelines.

**Expedition goals:** Cooled slag keys; fire-ward gear; Brogi cult alliances; embersteel prototypes for Cindervault patrons.

**Machine appetite:** **Corrupted distinctions** — duplicates, absorbed identities, tools mistaken for people (Hollowforged echoes).

**Gate Guardian:** **Ashthirst the Hollow**

**Key sites:** Hammerfall Hall (F21) · Molten River Crossing (F22) · Chained Anvil Chamber (F24) · Gearspark Forge (F27) · Lavaflow Gate (F30)

**Adventure seeds**

1. **Two Anvils** — A Hollowforged claims to be a lost party member; Gearspark runes say otherwise.  
2. **Slag Key Race** — Only one cooled slag shard remains before the Lavaflow Gate superheats; two allied Expeditions (12 seats each) share a camp — coalition rules apply.  
3. **Brogi's Tithe** — Cindervault smiths demand first claim on gate loot; the party's paladin hears Choir doctrine calling tithe **theft**.

---

### Tier 4 — Conflict · Isles of Eternal Radiance (Floors 31–40)

**Principle:** Difference becomes **opposition** — territorial struggle, predation, war.

**Culture & travel:** Floating dawn-isles over glowing mist; crystal bridges shift; radiant elementals replace Automaton patrols. Sun-shields mandatory.

**Expedition goals:** Sun-shard vials; dawn-glow tonics; Radiance Gate keys; mirror-reef intelligence (military value).

**Machine appetite:** **Unresolved war** — skirmishes that never conclude, radiant sects that schism rather than reconcile.

**Gate Guardian:** **Gor Vekk, Architect of Ends**

**Key sites:** Prismatic Archipelago (F31) · Mirror Reef (F34) · Sunforged Obelisk (F36) · Dawn's Refuge (F37) · Radiance Gate (F40)

**Adventure seeds**

1. **Mirror Commander** — Mirror Reef spawns a duplicate expedition commander who orders a suicidal bridge crossing.  
2. **Architect Cult** — Gor Vekk worshippers offer passage if the party **outlaws** a rival god's rites on the surface after victory — a Cycle consequence in miniature.  
3. **Zenith Failure** — Radiance Gate requires dawn zenith alignment; the horn falls on Day 50 **during** the attempt — retreat or miss the window?

---

### Tier 5 — Motion · Sanctum of Echoing Choirs (Floors 41–50)

**Principle:** Stagnation forbidden — geography shifts, rivers reverse, settlements migrate.

**Culture & travel:** Living-sound cathedral; whispers become symphonies. **Most expeditions end here.** Bar of Last Refuge (F43) is the pilgrimage turn-back shrine. Closer to Canticle Gate, calm and dread mix.

**Expedition goals:** Echo samples for Luminalis; Canticle Gate harmonic keys; Choir relics; **prestige** — surviving to Floor 50 before horn.

**Machine appetite:** **Forced movement** — parties that camp too long find havens relocated; stillness becomes lethal.

**Gate Guardian:** **Choralith, Voice Below**

**Key sites:** Whispering Pillars (F41) · Aeolian Mirror Pools (F42) · Bar of Last Refuge (F43) · Cantor's Archive (F47) · Canticle Gate (F50)

**Adventure seeds**
