# Decennium Descent player promise — review candidate

**Prepared for a later notebook review; not a replacement for the owner-locked Design Doctrine or approved books.** This consolidates the existing doctrine and Gameplay Loop1.2. It introduces no new mechanics. Tone, lethality targets, expected session length and expected campaign length still need comparison with the setting and Director sources before this charter can be closed.

Decennium Descent is a fantasy tabletop role-playing game about organizing an expedition, venturing into the dungeon during the decennial Opening, and bringing people, discoveries and valuable cargo back to the Haven. Tactical encounters matter because they consume time, health, equipment and supplies that the expedition also needs for its return. Recovering something valuable is only part of success: the company must carry it, survive extraction and settle its obligations afterward.

Players make consequential choices at three scales. In encounters, they use explicit actions, movement and class abilities. During eight-hour expedition Watches, they assign characters to combat, reconnaissance, research, resource or support work, track the route and manage attrition. In Haven and downtime, they resolve claims, sale, shares, debts, recovery, crafting, research and faction consequences. The Cycle Record carries the Opening's results into the next one.

A successful withdrawal, a completed objective and a profitable expedition are separate results. A retreat can preserve the company even when a contract fails. A victorious encounter or recovered treasure does not guarantee a successful expedition. The charter's casualty, reserve-supply and return-time limits make those choices concrete.

The design priority is the game's own expedition, extraction, Haven, Opening and seasonal identity; then tactical clarity and balance discipline; then simpler wording and table procedure. A rule should make its cost, trigger, target, duration and outcome explicit and remain understandable after one reading. It must not create unlimited action, summon, control, healing or resource loops. Each feature should contribute to combat, exploration, extraction, Haven play or seasonal consequences.

The intended authored deliverable is a coherent campaign-playtest candidate with usable characters, procedures, encounters and player/Director references. Reference checks establish specified rule behavior; numerical balance and ease of teaching still require actual human sessions.
