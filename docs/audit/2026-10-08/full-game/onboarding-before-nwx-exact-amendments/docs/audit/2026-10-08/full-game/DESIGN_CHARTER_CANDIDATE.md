# Decennium Descent player promise — review candidate

**Source-grounded draft awaiting joint notebook review.** This consolidates the owner-locked Design Doctrine, the existing gameplay loop, the Player Rulebook introduction and current character foundation. It introduces no new mechanics and does not replace those sources. The description of intended play below is an editorial synthesis for review; numerical balance and human readability remain unverified.

Decennium Descent is a fantasy tabletop role-playing game about organizing an expedition, venturing into the dungeon during the decennial Opening, and bringing people, discoveries and valuable cargo back to the Haven. Tactical encounters matter because they consume time, health, equipment and supplies that the expedition also needs for its return. Recovering something valuable is only part of success: the company must carry it, survive extraction and settle its obligations afterward.

Players make consequential choices at three scales. In encounters, they use explicit actions, movement and class abilities. During eight-hour expedition Watches, they assign characters to combat, reconnaissance, research, resource or support work, track the route and manage attrition. In Haven and downtime, they resolve claims, sale, shares, debts, recovery, crafting, research and faction consequences. The Cycle Record carries the Opening's results into the next one.

A successful withdrawal, a completed objective and a profitable expedition are separate results. A retreat can preserve the company even when a contract fails. A victorious encounter or recovered treasure does not guarantee a successful expedition. The charter's casualty, reserve-supply and return-time limits make those choices concrete.

The design priority is the game's own expedition, extraction, Haven, Opening and seasonal identity; then tactical clarity and balance discipline; then simpler wording and table procedure. A rule should make its cost, trigger, target, duration and outcome explicit and remain understandable after one reading. It must not create unlimited action, summon, control, healing or resource loops. Each feature should contribute to combat, exploration, extraction, Haven play or seasonal consequences.

The intended experience combines capable adventurers, discovery and meaningful character choices with danger that can leave lasting costs. Cooperation, negotiation, preparation and retreat are all useful play. Character advancement expands the available choices: Base classes establish the starting identity, with Arch and Hero choices reached through their actual class schedules. Opening campaigns use announced milestones; optional XP remains a separate table choice. Advancement does not erase spent resources or heal existing injuries.

The rules support tactical encounters within a longer expedition and campaign. They do not require every session to contain every part of that cycle. Agree the session length, breaks, attendance arrangements and desired emphasis at session zero. Neither the decennial fictional calendar nor the number of dungeon floors specifies a real-world session count. This charter promises explicit consequences and meaningful withdrawal choices, not a guaranteed survival rate or a fixed number of sessions to level 20.

The intended authored deliverable is a coherent campaign-playtest candidate with usable characters, procedures, encounters and player/Director references. Reference checks establish specified rule behavior; numerical balance and ease of teaching still require actual human sessions.

## Questions every major subsystem must answer

| System | Test against this promise |
|---|---|
| Characters, classes, ancestry, backgrounds, skills and feats | Does each legal choice give a clear contribution, with its acquisition stage and actual prerequisites visible? |
| Combat, conditions, magic and companions | Can players resolve costs, timing, control and consequences without creating extra actions or resource loops? |
| Exploration, information, social scenes and factions | Can knowledge, preparation and negotiation change a decision while preserving uncertainty, agency and source limits? |
| Health, rest, supplies, equipment and transport | Do injuries, time and carrying limits affect the next decision, including whether the group can return? |
| Crafting, harvesting, cargo and Haven economy | Can the table trace actual inputs, work, output, custody and settlement without counting resources or benefits twice? |
| Monsters, encounters and adventures | Are threats, objectives, retreat routes and failure consequences playable, with provisional balance assumptions identified? |
| Advancement, Opening and campaign continuity | Does the record preserve earned progress and unresolved consequences through the existing procedures? |
| Player references, Director tools and companion interfaces | Can people find the governing rule and keep the same state on paper or in software, within the proper audience? |
